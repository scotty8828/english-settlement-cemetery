Biography

Richard Christopher Harrison was born 23 May 1838 in Pennsylvania, son of Benjamin Inskip Harrison and Nancy Brown Harrison, both already documented on this site. His mother died 9 September 1840, six days after the birth of his youngest brother, Edward Inskip Harrison — Richard was two years old. His father later remarried, and Richard had at least one younger half-brother from that second marriage, George Matthew Harrison (1855–1926). Richard's known full siblings included Jane Harrison Morris, Benjamin Harrison, John Brown Harrison, Elizabeth "Betsy" Harrison Gilson, and Ellen Harrison Morris, all already documented on this site.

During the Civil War, Richard served as a private in Company H of the 57th Pennsylvania Regiment. He enlisted 2 September 1861 and was discharged 1 November 1864, a service of about three years and two months. His younger brother, Edward Inskip Harrison, served in the same company. This record comes from the 1890 Special Schedule of Surviving Soldiers, Sailors, and Marines for Rome Township — no gravestone, obituary, or other source reviewed for this page mentions his military service at all.

By about 1872, Richard had married Clarissa V. Hicks, already documented on this site. Their first child, Charlotte Mae "Lottie" Harrison Hopkins, was born 14 August 1873 in Rome Township, followed by Ruland A. Harrison (1881–1965) and Edith Esma Harrison Dougan (1889–1966).

Clarissa died in 1909 of stomach cancer. Richard died two years later, on 15 September 1911, of the same disease — his death certificate, like hers, records the cause as cancer of the stomach, and both were attended in their final illness by the same physician, Dr. E. E. Maitland of Spartansburg. His daughter Edith served as informant on his death certificate; she recorded her own grandmother Nancy Brown Harrison's maiden name as "not known," even though it is independently documented elsewhere on this site. Richard was buried two days after his death, on 17 September 1911, at what his own death certificate records simply as "Vroman" — a further primary-source confirmation of that alternate name for this cemetery.
