Biography

Elizabeth "Betsy" Harrison was born 19 March 1826 in England, daughter of Benjamin Inskip Harrison (1797–1875) and his first wife, Nancy Brown Harrison (1794–1840), both already documented on this site. She came to America with her parents at about age two. Her own obituary states that her father bought "the farm now owned by S. S. Harrison" around 1833, when it was "then almost a wilderness." When Betsy was thirteen, her mother died, leaving her, as the eldest of the household's children, with "the care of a large family of small children" — an account consistent with Nancy Brown Harrison's own documented death in 1840.

About 1847 she married Richard Brown Gilson (1813–1893), also buried at English Settlement Cemetery. A county history, Our County and Its People, records that Richard was born in Tidioute, Pennsylvania — giving his birth year as 1814 and his death year as 1894, both a year off from this site's own record — the son of John Gilson and Anna (Bell) Gilson. Richard and Betsy settled on a farm in Rome Township, later inherited by their son William. Richard predeceased Betsy "by several years."

Betsy and Richard had seven children, all living at the time of her death: John Harrison Gilson (1847–1932) of Gilson Ridge; Benjamin Harrison Gilson (1849–1931), also buried at English Settlement Cemetery, who worked as an oil field driller and sawmill owner in Titusville and was elected justice of the peace in 1893; Christopher C. Gilson (1851–1921); Nancy Ann Gilson Coates (1853–1936) of Chase Ridge; Richard Theodore "Thede" Gilson (1856–1913), who married Emily Viola Coates; George B. Gilson (1864–1954) of Eagle; and William Brown Gilson (1866–1943), who inherited the family farm.

Her siblings included Jane Harrison Morris (1818–1885), Benjamin Harrison (1822–1901), John Brown Harrison (1824–1905), Thomas Harrison (1829–1906), Sarah Harrison Harrison (1830–1887), Ellen Harrison Morris (1836–1906), Richard Christopher Harrison (1838–1911), and Edward Inskip Harrison (1840–1914), most already documented on this site. A half-brother, George Matthew Harrison (1855–1926), was born to their father's third wife, Isabella Nesbitt Harrison, also already documented. Her own obituary names her one living sister as "Mrs. Edward Morris, of Vroman" — identifying Ellen Harrison Morris's husband as Edward Morris, per this site's already-published page for Richard Brown Gilson.

Elizabeth "Betsy" Harrison Gilson died 26 March 1904 at her home in Rome Township, at the age of 78, and was buried at English Settlement Cemetery.

Additional biography will be added as further research clarifies the "Mrs. Edward Morris" reference and the Gilson family's farm history. If you have information about Elizabeth "Betsy" Harrison Gilson, please use the contribution form below.

Primary source — obituary (handwritten transcription), publication not stated

"Mrs. Betsy Harrison Gilson, died at her home March 26. She was born in England March 1826, came with her parents to this country at the age of two years. Her father bought the farm now owned by S. S. Harrison about 1833 then almost a wilderness. When she was thirteen her mother died leaving her with the care of a large family of small children. About 1847 she was married to Richard Gilson who preceded her to the grave by several years. She was the mother of seven children all living, John, of Gilson Ridge, B. H. of Titusville, Christopher, R. T. and George of Eagle and Willie of this place and Mrs. Clint Coates, of Chase Ridge, also five brothers John Harrison, Thomas, Richard, E. J. of this place and George of Goodwill Hill and one sister, Mrs. Edward Morris, of Vroman."

— Handwritten obituary transcription, as reproduced on Find A Grave
Secondary source — Our County and Its People (county history)

"Richard B Gilson, of Rome township, is a son of John and Anna (Bell) Gilson, and was born in Tidioute, Pennsylvania, in 1814. His father was an early settler in Oil Creek township. He married Betsy Harrison, daughter of Benjamin Harrison, of Rome Township. He settled there on the farm now owned by his son William, was a farmer and had seven children, — John H, Benjamin H., Christopher C., Theodore R., Nancy A., George B., and William B. He died in 1894."

— Our County and Its People, as reproduced via Ancestry.com
