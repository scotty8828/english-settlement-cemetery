Biography

James A. Vroman was born 22 February 1824 in New York, a son of James J. Vrooman (1799–1869) and Mary "Polly" Rendola (1795–1876). This birth year is confirmed in a published genealogy, The Vrooman Family in America: Descendants of Hendrick Meese Vrooman Who Came From Holland To America in 1664, which resolves a discrepancy between his own headstone (carved 1811, and flagged as inaccurate by Find A Grave's own research) and Find A Grave's alternate estimate of 1821 — both of which are superseded here by the book's more specific and internally consistent date.

According to a family account provided by descendant George L. Vroman, James journeyed as a child with his parents and siblings Frances B. (b.1827), Eliza Ann (b.1829), and newborn sister Mary E. (b.1833), from New York to Eldred Township, Warren County, Pennsylvania, in 1833 — consistent with the published genealogy's note that James J. Vrooman "migrated from New York State to Pennsylvania in 1833." A younger brother, Orin B. Vroman, was born 23 May 1834 in Pennsylvania and died 18 September 1859. The family eventually settled on a thirty-acre farm at the northwest corner of Farrington and Vroman roads in Rome Township, Crawford County — the Vroman Road name still marking the family's presence in the area. James's parents sold this farm to Richard B. Harrison on 24 November 1866 for $300, a deed recorded in the Crawford County records office on 6 March 1868.

James married Mary Ellen Harrison (1817–1898), daughter of Richard Harrison and Mary Ann Malley, already documented on this site. A surviving letter from Jane Harrison Morris to her husband Richard Morris describes the wedding directly: it was a Christmas Day double wedding, with Mary Ellen's sister Elizabeth Harrison marrying Henry Wheatall on the same day, both officiated by the Presbyterian minister of Titusville. James and Mary Ellen had at least two daughters: Mary Ellen Vroman Owens (1846–1871), who died young and is also buried here, and Elmira Amelia Vroman Morris (1850–1915), who later lived at Buell's Corners.

James A. Vroman died 1 November 1904 in the English Settlement, survived by his daughter Elmira and a brother, Francis Vroman of Excelsior. His funeral, held at Vroman, was conducted by Rev. Mr. Meeker of Grand Valley; "Nearer, My God, to Thee" was sung. His obituary states plainly that "interment was made in the Vroman cemetery" — a direct, period use of that name for this cemetery. His pallbearers were C. Harrison, E. Harrison, J. Harrison, George Farrington, John Stall, and John Morris.

Additional biography will be added as the birth year and death date discrepancies are resolved. If you have information about James A. Vroman, please use the contribution form below.

Primary source — obituary, Titusville Morning Herald, 18 November 1904

"Services in memory of James A Vrooman (Vroman), who died in the English Settlement, Nov 1, 1904, at 3 o'clock in the afternoon were held at Vroman Thursday, at 2 o'clock. Mr. Vroman is survived by one daughter, Mrs. Elmira Morris of Buell, Pa., and one brother, Francis Vrooman (sic) Vroman of Excelsior. There was a large circle of sorrowing relatives and friends present. The floral offerings were carnations and chrysanthemums. The services at the church were in charge of Rev Mr. Meeker of Grand Valley. Mr. Vrooman (sic) Vroman was born in Jefferson, NY, 83 years ago, and at the age of 24 came to Pennsylvania and married Mary Harrison of Paterson, NJ, in good and regular standing of the First Free Independent Presbyterian church in Paterson, NJ. Mr. Vroman lived a good Christian life and will be missed by a large circle of friends. Miss Ida Steward, Mr. Will Gregory and Mrs. Samuel Stewart sang 'Nearer, My God, to Thee.' Interment was made in the Vroman cemetery, the following named gentlemen acting as pallbearers: C. Harrison, E. Harrison, J. Harrison, George Farrington, John Stall, John Morris."

— Titusville Morning Herald, 18 November 1904, p.6, as reproduced on Find A Grave
Primary source — personal letter, Jane Harrison Morris to Richard Morris (undated)

"On Christmas Day I went to the wedding of brother Richards 2 daughters, Mary to James Vroman and Elizabeth to Henry Wheatall, who bought the place your father used to live on, they were married by the Presbyterian minister of Titusville, there was a large company of us chiefly relations. Me and Edward was at the Vroman's the next day at the infare. Inskip and Billy Harrison called in today as they were going to the meeting. Joseph Barnsdale preached the sermon and Mr. Post a Morman another, the Morman's are next to the Universalists in liberality of the mind. Brother Richard and Molly Margaret and her baby and the Vroman's were all at the meeting today they came in their sleighs."

— Family letter, as provided in research materials for this site

Birth year resolved: 1824. A published, cited genealogy — The Vrooman Family in America — gives 22 February 1824, resolving the earlier discrepancy between his headstone (1811, flagged inaccurate) and Find A Grave's own estimate (1821). This site's master record should be updated to 1824.
Death date resolved: 1 November 1904. Three independent sources — Find A Grave, his obituary, and the published Vrooman genealogy — all agree on 1 November. This site's own master record instead shows 11 November 1904, which appears to be a data-entry error and should be corrected.
Obituary's emigration claim conflicts with the family account. The obituary states he "at the age of 24 came to Pennsylvania," but the family account has him arriving as a 9-year-old with his parents in 1833. The obituary reporter may have conflated his age at marriage with his age at emigration. Not resolved.
Strongest "Vroman cemetery" corroboration yet. His own 1904 obituary states directly that "interment was made in the Vroman cemetery," and a separate Find A Grave contributor note independently states "Burial In Maple Grove Cemetery aka (The English Settlement Cemetery)." Combined with the still-standing Vroman Road in Rome Township, this is the most direct primary-source evidence yet for this cemetery's alternate names. Per standing instruction, not yet written into about.html — flagged here only.
Marriage location worth checking against Mary Ellen's own page. The obituary states the marriage took place in Paterson, NJ, at "the First Free Independent Presbyterian church" — geographically unusual for a family otherwise centered in Crawford County. Worth confirming against Mary Ellen Harrison Vroman's own published page (#066) for consistency.
Naming variant. The published genealogy calls his daughter "Almira," matching this site's "Elmira Amelia Vroman Morris" (#104). Likely the same person under a spelling variant, not a separate individual.
