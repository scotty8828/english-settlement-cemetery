Biography

Early Life & Emigration

Richard Morris was born in Lancashire, England, in July 1810, son of James Morris (1782–1858) and Ann Morris (1790–1863) — a Morris family that would itself emigrate and settle at Rome Township, where his parents and a brother, James, are buried alongside him at English Settlement Cemetery. Amid the economic shifts of the Industrial Revolution, the wider family joined the wave of English emigrants who left for America in the 1830s. On 23 April 1830, at the Second Reformed Church in Wyckoff, New Jersey, he married Jane Harrison — a fellow Lancashire native and skilled power-loom operator. Together, they began building their family in the Northeast.

Settling in Crawford County

Looking to move beyond the industrial mill centers, Richard and Jane, along with her brothers Benjamin and Inskip Harrison, purchased 400 acres of land in Crawford County, Pennsylvania — wilderness land of virgin pine forest. Over the coming decades, Richard worked to clear the dense timberland and establish a prosperous family farm. In this close-knit frontier community, he and Jane raised six sons to adulthood: Edward, James, William, Benjamin, John, and Inskip.

Civil War & Family Tragedy

The Civil War brought profound personal heartache to the Morris household. In 1862, against Richard's wishes, their second son, James, enlisted in a Pennsylvania volunteer regiment. He was mortally wounded during the Battle of Gettysburg in July 1863 and died of his wounds weeks later in New York. Driven by a deep reverence for her son, Jane — without her husband's knowledge — made secret arrangements to have James's body brought home. He was laid to rest with the rest of the family at English Settlement Cemetery. His full story is told in a dedicated biography.

Legacy

Richard Morris died on 19 May 1871, at the age of 60. He was buried at English Settlement Cemetery, sharing a memorial with his wife Jane, where his headstone stands as an enduring monument to the mid-19th-century British immigrants who helped found the local community.

Richard Morris's parents and a brother are already documented burials at English Settlement Cemetery, meaning the Morris family emigrated from Lancashire in their own right, not solely through marriage into the Harrisons — directly relevant to the project's question of whether these families knew one another before crossing the Atlantic. Several open items: the 1833 land purchase is not yet backed by a deed (a later family memoir cites 1845, but this conflicts with documented family movements and is not used here); son William S. Morris has not been matched with confidence — the master cemetery record shows a "William S. Morris Jr." with different birth/death dates (1837–1915) than Ancestry's William S. Morris (1842–1891), and these may not be the same person; and an alternate cemetery name from the memoir ("Old English Settlement Cemetery / Maple Grove Cemetery") has not been confirmed and is not used on this site. Son James's own biography, covering Gettysburg and his return home, is planned separately.
