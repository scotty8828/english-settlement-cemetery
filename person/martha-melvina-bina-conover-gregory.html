Biography

Martha Melvina "Bina" Conover was born 24 May 1858 at Vrooman, Crawford County, Pennsylvania, the daughter of Garrett Bonum Conover (1818–1903) and Eliza Ann Thompson Conover, who had married 5 June 1845. Garrett was born in Hunterdon County, New Jersey, one of twelve children of Garret A. and Sarah Conover, who brought their family to Rome Township on 8 November 1832, when the township was still largely wilderness. One of Garrett's brothers, Ralph Conover, served in the 18th Pennsylvania Volunteer Cavalry during the Civil War and was killed at Brandy Station, on the Potomac; Garrett and Eliza Ann took in Ralph's orphaned son, Elliott E. Conover, and raised him alongside their own children.

Martha was one of at least seven children — John Garrett Conover (1849–1936), George Bonum Conover (1851–1919), Emma Jane Conover Jones (1853–1918), Frances "Fannie" M. Conover Stewart (1854–1898), Cora Catherine "Kate" Conover McClintock (1862–1929), and Elizabeth "Libbie" Conover Morris (1867–1939), also documented on this site. Before her marriage, Martha worked as a schoolteacher at Grand Valley, Warren County. On 30 August 1884, she married William Valentine Gregory, not yet built on this site but already numbered in the cemetery master record, and the couple settled and spent their married life at Vrooman.

Martha and William had six sons. Five survived her: Garrett (Alfred Garrett Gregory, 1885–1965), Edwin, Christopher (Christopher Conover Gregory, 1887–1927), Percival, and Jesse (Jesse Howell Gregory, 1898–1984). Their sixth son, Benjamin Carlisle Gregory (1895–1898), already documented on this site, died at not quite three years old.

At Vrooman, Martha was an active member of the United Presbyterian church, which she had joined in her youth. She served for years as the church organist, taught a Sunday school class, and was elected president of the local Woman's Christian Temperance Union chapter. She died at Corry Hospital on 14 October 1912, at fifty-four, after a seven-month illness. Her obituary records that the day before she died, in a moment free of her suffering, her mind returned to the church she loved, and she asked after the work of "the little congregation." Her remains were brought back to Vrooman, and her sons Christopher and Percival returned from Greenville for the funeral.

Obituary
Newspaper obituary, October 1912 (Titusville Herald, per file name "1912.Bina.Conover.Gregory.Obit") · "Mrs. W. V. Gregory Has Passed Away — The End Came Monday at Corry Hospital"
"Mrs. Bina Gregory, wife of W. V. Gregory, one of the best known and respected residents of Vrooman, died at the Corry Hospital Monday morning at 12:20 o'clock, following an illness of seven months' duration. The news arrived at Vrooman on Monday and was a deep shock to all who knew the deceased. Mrs. Gregory was the daughter of Bonum and Eliza Conover and was born at Vrooman, May 24, 1858. All her life was spent at Vrooman and on Aug. 30, 1884, she was united in marriage to W. V. Gregory. To this union six sons were born. Five of them survive to comfort their father in the hour of his sorrow. They are Garrett of Vrooman, Edwin, Christopher and Percival, of Greenville, and Jesse, who lives at home and is now a student in Titusville high school. Mrs. Gregory is also survived by the following brothers and sisters: George Conover of Spartansburg, and John Conover, Mrs. Emma Jones, Mrs. Kate McClintock and Mrs. Libbie Morris, all of Vrooman. Mrs. Gregory was a kind and devoted wife and mother, a neighbor to be prized and a church worker whose zeal was measured only by her strength. She was for years an organist in the United Presbyterian church of Vrooman, with which she united in her youth, and for a long time taught a class in the Sunday school. In the W.C.T.U. she was for many years an efficient worker and was for years elected president of the local union at Vrooman. The day before she died she forgot her sufferings for a moment and her mind went back to the church she loved so well and she inquired for the work of the little congregation. The remains were brought to Vrooman from Corry on Monday and her sons came home from Greenville last night. No arrangements have been made so far for the funeral services, but it is thought that they will be held Wednesday afternoon."
