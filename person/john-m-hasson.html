Biography

John M. Hasson was born 17 April 1839 in Philadelphia County, Pennsylvania, and lived there until the outbreak of the Civil War. In 1861 he enlisted to serve in Company D of the 5th Pennsylvania Cavalry, a service that his own gravestone still records today. He served three years and eighty-two days.

After the war, John came to the Rome Township area. At about the age of thirty, around 1869, he married Mary Ann Harrison of Red Oak. She was the daughter of William Harrison and Sarah Harrison Harrison, both already documented on this site. John Hasson himself was not born into the extended Harrison family, but he married into a household that was, by this generation, deeply intermarried: Mary Ann's own parents, William Harrison and Sarah Harrison Harrison, were themselves first cousins, both grandchildren of Benjamin Harrison and Jane Inskip Harrison.

John and Mary Ann had eight children together — five girls and three boys. Two, Nettie and George, died before their father. Only one child is currently identified by name and dates in the records reviewed for this page: their daughter Esther Carroll Hasson, later Root (1882–1946), whose own 1946 death certificate independently confirms her parents as John Hasson and Mary Harrison. The other six children are not yet documented by name.

About ten years before his death, John and his family moved to a farm near Lincolnville, where he lived out the rest of his life. He was a farmer and, as his obituary records, "a prominent Odd Fellow, who believed and practiced the principles of F. L. T., together with the golden rule" — a reference to Friendship, Love, and Truth, the guiding motto of the Independent Order of Odd Fellows. He was prostrated for about seven weeks with paralysis before he died at his Lincolnville home on 7 February 1905. His funeral was held at the United Presbyterian church in Little Cooley, Rev. Reed presiding, and he was buried in what a contemporary account of his own funeral called "the Vroman cemetery" — one of the several historical names by which this cemetery has been known.

Obituaries
Titusville Herald · 9 February 1905 · p. 4 · "Little Cooley" correspondence
"Little Cooley, Feb. 8. — John Hasson, a resident of Bloomfield, formerly of Rome township, died at his late home yesterday morning where he has been prostrated about seven weeks with paralysis. He was aged about 60 years. He leaves a family of two sons and four daughters, all married except one, who resides at home. The remains will be taken to his former home for burial. He had many friends here, for to know Mr. Hasson was to respect and honor him. He was a loving husband and kind father and a prominent Odd Fellow, who believed and practiced the principles of F. L. T., together with the golden rule."
Newspaper obituary, February 1905 (publication not yet identified) · clipping via Find A Grave, added by 4ever a Young
"John Hason, an old resident of this place passed away at his home at Lincolnville, Feb. 7. Mr. Hason lived here for many years and was respected by all who knew him. The deceased was born in Philadelphia, April 17, 1839, where he lived until 1861 when he enlisted to serve his country, where he served three years and 82 days. Then he came to this neighborhood and at the age of thirty he was married to Miss Marian Harrison, of Red Oak, to them were born eight children, five girls and three boys, Nettie and George having preceded him to the great beyond. About ten years ago he moved onto a farm near Lincolnville where he lived until his death. He leaves to mourn the loss, a wife and six children. The remains were brought here Thursday for burial; services were held at the U.P. church, Rev. Reed, of Little Cooley, preached the sermon. Interment was made in the Vroman cemetery."

Wife's name recorded two ways: One obituary clipping records her as "Miss Marian Harrison, of Red Oak"; Find A Grave and her daughter's 1946 death certificate both record her as Mary Ann Harrison. Almost certainly the same woman, recorded differently by two sources.
Married into the family, not born into it: Mary Ann Harrison Hasson was the daughter of William Harrison and Sarah Harrison Harrison — a first-cousin marriage in the prior generation, already documented on this site. Neither Mary Ann nor the couple's known daughter Esther is buried at English Settlement Cemetery; both have no page on this site.
"Vroman cemetery" — a period source for one of this site's alternate names: The second obituary's own account of John's burial, "interment was made in the Vroman cemetery," is a contemporary, primary-source use of this cemetery's alternate name, corroborating the multiple historical names for English Settlement Cemetery already documented on this site.
