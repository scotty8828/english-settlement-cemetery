Biography

Jane Inskip was born in late 1766 in Yorkshire, England, and baptized on Christmas Day, 25 December 1766, at the Salterforth Inghamite Chapel near Bolton-by-Bowland. She was the daughter of Richard Inskip (1724–1807), a yeoman farmer and trustee of the Independent Chapel at Holden, and Elizabeth Frankland. The Inskip family's roots ran deep into the Yorkshire soil — land records in the Slaidburn Court Rolls trace the family back to the mid-1600s at Smalden. Jane grew up in a household shaped by faith, farming, and the self-reliance of the English dales.

On 27 July 1784, at eighteen years old, Jane married Benjamin Harrison at Bolton-by-Bowland. Benjamin worked initially as a butcher, but as their family grew the Harrisons settled into Lancashire, a region fast becoming the heart of England's textile industry. Over the next twenty-seven years Jane and Benjamin raised a large family there — Richard, Anne, Eleanor, Matthew, Elizabeth, Benjamin Inskip, Jane, Edward, and Inskip. Jane herself became a skilled handloom weaver, a trade she passed down to her daughters. When her father died in 1807, his will named her directly, leaving a bequest to "Jane the wife of Benjamin Harrison."

By the 1820s, the rise of mechanized looms was putting immense pressure on Lancashire's handloom-weaving families. Benjamin Harrison died in May 1824, and Jane — now widowed — faced a life-altering decision. In 1827, at approximately sixty years of age, she gathered seven of her children and made the transatlantic crossing to the United States, leaving behind the only world she had known. The family settled first in Paterson, New Jersey, remaining there for several years. In 1833, Jane — together with her sons Benjamin Inskip Harrison and Inskip Harrison, and her son-in-law Richard Morris, husband of her daughter Jane — purchased 400 acres in Rome Township, Crawford County, and moved into the Pennsylvania wilderness, where they helped establish what became known as the English Settlement.

Jane spent her final years surrounded by her children, grandchildren, and fellow English pioneers. The 1840 census shows her living in the household of her son Benjamin Harrison, with the homesteads of her other children close by. She died on 13 October 1845, aged 78 (her headstone traditionally records "Aged 84 Years"), and was among the earliest burials at what would become English Settlement Cemetery. Her stone, topped with a square marble crown, bears an epitaph that speaks to a woman who led her family across an ocean to build a new home:

"Farewell my children one and all / I bid adieu to young and old / I have long lived with you on earth / And now return back to dust."

The name she carried into marriage — Inskip — was not forgotten. It passed to her son Inskip Harrison, one of the foundational figures of the Rome Township community, then to his son Edward Inskip Harrison, and eventually into the Morris family through marriage, appearing as late as Harold Inskip Morris in the twentieth century. Jane Inskip Harrison may never have known how far her name would travel.

Jane Inskip Harrison is the earliest documented birth in English Settlement Cemetery (1766) and is almost certainly the origin of the Inskip name carried across four generations of the Harrison and Morris families. Her years in Lancashire's handloom-weaving trade place her family within the same textile-district migration wave as several other Lancashire-origin families in this cemetery, supporting the project's broader question of whether these families knew one another before crossing the Atlantic. The family's path to Rome Township ran through Paterson, New Jersey (1827–1833) before the 1833 land purchase, made alongside her sons Benjamin Inskip Harrison and Inskip Harrison and her son-in-law Richard Morris. Her daughters Elizabeth (Harrison Gregory, 1794–1881) and Jane (Harrison Morris, 27 Jan 1806 – 2 Jan 1887) are now confirmed — Jane shares a memorial with Richard Morris. Anne, Eleanor, Matthew, and Edward have not yet been matched to a cemetery record.
