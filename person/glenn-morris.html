Biography

On 1 August 1914, at their home at 554 W. Church Street in Corry, Pennsylvania, Floyd Ira Morris and his wife Flossie Heliker Morris lost a child at birth. The attending physician, Dr. O. Darcell Magouren, recorded the death that same day, listing the cause plainly on the certificate as "still born child." The infant was buried within hours, that same afternoon, at Harrison Cemetery in Rome Township, Crawford County — the undertaking handled by Alexander & Co. of Corry.

The death certificate itself gives no first name; the child is recorded only as "Child of Mr. & Mrs. Floyd J. Morris." The name Glenn comes down through family memory and Find A Grave rather than the official record — a common pattern for stillborn infants of this era, who were often not formally named on state paperwork even when a name had been chosen or was given afterward by the family.

Floyd Ira Morris and Flossie Heliker Morris went on to have at least four more children who survived to adulthood: George L. Morris (1916–1930), Edith Jean Morris White (1930–2009), and twins Roberta I. "Betty" Morris McIntyre and Robert Ira Morris (both 1933–2020). Glenn's brief place in the family is remembered on this page alongside his siblings.

Glenn's paternal grandfather was John Morris (1845–1928), whose first wife, Saraph Fish Morris (1859–1895), is also buried at this cemetery. Saraph died in 1895 when Floyd Ira — her son and Glenn's father — was seven years old. John Morris later remarried, to Elizabeth "Libbie" Conover. Floyd Ira Morris was a child of John Morris's first marriage, to Saraph Fish.
