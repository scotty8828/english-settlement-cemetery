Biography

Iphigenia Jean "Effie" Wheatall was born 3 July 1847 in Rome Township, Crawford County, Pennsylvania, the daughter of Henry Wheatall (1819–1891) and Elizabeth Harrison Wheatall (1823–1883). Her unusual first name came from her paternal grandmother, Iphigenia Dodd Wheatall, a Londoner. Her mother, Elizabeth, was a sister of Ellen Harrison Harrison, already documented on this site — making Iphigenia a niece of Ellen and her husband, John Brown Harrison, also documented on this site. Iphigenia had at least six siblings: Henry Richard Wheatall (1850–1930), William Edward Wheatall (1853–1917), Benjamin J. Wheatall (1846–1925, named for his late uncle), and three sisters — Eugenia, Lillian, and Adelaide — all of whom died within eight days of one another in February 1873, ages seventeen, fourteen, and eleven, in a documented diphtheria epidemic.

In 1871, Iphigenia married Benjamin Morris (1840–1915), a brother of Edward Morris, already documented on this site. This was Benjamin's second marriage — his first wife, Lucy Ann Sedden, had died young, in 1869, and their son, Frederick Wilson Morris (1867–1938), was raised in large part by his grandmother, Jane Harrison Morris, also documented on this site. Frederick appears in some family trees alongside Iphigenia's own children, but he was her stepson, not her biological child. Iphigenia and Benjamin had at least four children of their own: Herbert Richard Morris (1883–1963), Rupert E. Morris (1885–1890), also documented on this site, Edna J. Morris Foley (1886–1941), and Clyde Nugent Morris (1891–1959).

In her final months, Iphigenia and Benjamin moved to Spartansburg. Her health failed there, and she died at her home on West Main Street on 15 January 1912, at about sixty-three. Her death certificate records the cause as pernicious anemia; her obituary, in less clinical language, called it "dropsy." She was buried three days later, on 18 January 1912, at what her own death certificate records as "Vrooman," following a funeral at the Maple Grove Church.

Obituary
Newspaper obituary, January 1912 (publication not yet identified) · via Find A Grave, added by JNicholson
"Effie Jean Morris, wife of Benjamin Morris, passed away at her home on West Main Street, Monday afternoon, January 15, 1912, aged about 63 years. She was born in Rome township. Miss Wheatall married Benjamin Morris, to whom three children came to this union, Bert, residing near Brimm's Corners; Clyde and Mrs. Edna Foley, residing on the old homestead, who with her husband, survive her. For the past few months Mr. and Mrs. Morris have resided in Spartansburg. Since coming here her health has been gradually failing and for some time there has been little hopes of her recovery. The immediate cause of death being dropsy. The funeral was held yesterday at the Maple Grove Church."

Resolved — Edward Morris's page: Edward Morris's brother, Benjamin Morris (1840–1915), Iphigenia's husband, was listed on Edward's page as "not yet confirmed buried here." He is in fact in the cemetery master record (#103), matching his documented dates exactly. Benjamin's own page is now built.
Birth year corrected to 1847: The cemetery master record and Find A Grave both give 1848, but her own death certificate and Bob Wheatall's sourced family research both give 3 July 1847. This page follows the better-supported 1847 date; the master record has not yet been updated to match.
Sourced from Bob Wheatall's family research: Much of the fuller Wheatall family story — the diphtheria epidemic that claimed three of Iphigenia's sisters, her father Henry's obituary, and the Frederick Morris chronology question — comes from "Descendants of Benjamin Wheattall and Iphigenia Dodd," compiled by Bob Wheatall (10 Aug 2026). A fuller telling of the whole Wheatall family, including members not buried at English Settlement Cemetery, is on this site's Wheatall family page.
Cause of death recorded two ways: Her death certificate, signed by a physician, gives the cause as pernicious anemia. Her obituary describes it as "dropsy" — a period lay term generally referring to fluid swelling rather than a blood disorder. Both are given here as documented, without resolving which better reflects the underlying condition.
Frederick W. Morris — chronology conflict now largely resolved: This page previously flagged a conflict: family research by Bob Wheatall placed Frederick as a son of Benjamin's first marriage, to Lucy Ann Sedden (who died in 1869), but an Ancestry family tree gave Frederick's birth year as 1887 — sixteen years after that marriage ended. Benjamin Morris's own page (built September 2026) draws on his Find A Grave family record, which gives Frederick's dates as 1867–1938 — a birth year that fits cleanly before Lucy Ann's 1869 death. This page has been updated to use 1867–1938. The earlier 1887–1958 pair from the Ancestry tree appears to have been in error.
