Biography

Mary Ellen Harrison was born 27 April 1817 in Lancashire, England, daughter of Richard Harrison and Mary Ann Malley Harrison, both already documented at length elsewhere on this site. This confirms a connection already suspected from other family charts examined earlier in this project's research: Mary Ellen was Richard's own daughter, not a direct daughter of her grandmother Jane Inskip Harrison, as she may have appeared in earlier, less complete records.

On Christmas Day, 25 December 1845, Mary Ellen married James A. Vroman in a double wedding at Titusville, officiated by the town's Presbyterian minister: her own sister, Elizabeth Harrison, married Henry Wheatall the same day. A letter written soon afterward by their aunt, Jane Harrison Morris, to her husband Richard Morris — both already documented on this site — describes the occasion in vivid detail: a large gathering of relations, an "infare" at the Vromans' the next day, and a passing note that Henry Wheatall had bought the very property "your father used to live on" — meaning James Morris's own old homestead. The letter is headed "Maple Grove, Rome township," independent confirmation of a name for the community already documented elsewhere on this site through several other sources.

Mary Ellen and James had at least two children: Mary Ellen Vroman Owens (1846–1871), already documented on this site, and Elmira Amelia Vroman Morris (1850–1915), who would go on to marry James "Jimmy" Morris and become the mother of Alfred Morris — also already documented on this site. Mary Ellen was, through this line, Alfred's own grandmother.

Mary Ellen Harrison Vroman died 3 March 1898 at eighty years old. She was buried at the English Settlement beneath a shared monument with her husband.

Primary source: a niece's letter
Letter from Jane Harrison Morris to her husband Richard Morris, headed "Maple Grove, Rome township," describing the Christmas Day 1845 wedding
"On Christmas Day I went to the wedding of brother Richard's daughters, Mary to James Vroman and Elizabeth to Henry Wheatall, who bought the place your father used to live on. They were married by the Presbyterian minister of Titusville; there was a large company of us, chiefly relations. Me and Edward was at the Vroman's the next day at the infare. Inskip and Billy Harrison called in today as they were going to the meeting. Joseph Barnsdale preached the sermon and Mr. Post, a Mormon, another — the Mormons are next to the Universalists in liberality of the mind. Brother Richard and Molly, Margaret and her baby, and the Vroman's were all at the meeting today; they came in their sleighs."

Resolves an earlier flagged question: A network chart examined on William Harrison's page raised the possibility that Mary Ellen was Richard Harrison's daughter rather than Jane Inskip Harrison's. This page's sources — Find A Grave and Ancestry both — confirm that directly: Mary Ellen is Richard and Mary Ann Malley Harrison's daughter. Worth revisiting Jane Inskip Harrison's own page with this in mind.
Husband's birth year in question: The monument itself reads "James A. Vroman, born Feb. 22, 1811," but Find A Grave's own family listing gives his dates as "1821–1904" — a ten-year discrepancy. Not resolved.
Marriage year — 1845 or 1846? Jane Harrison Morris's letter and other sources are specific: the wedding took place Christmas Day, 1845. Find A Grave's family panel instead lists "m. 1846." The more specific, dated source is used here.
Age at death possibly misread on the stone: Calculated from her documented birth and death dates, Mary Ellen was 80 at death, matching Find A Grave's own header. A photograph of the monument itself appears to show "aged 35 y.," almost certainly a worn or misread digit (most likely "85," itself one year off from the calculated 80, or simply illegible). Not resolved.
Possible earlier, same-named child: An Ancestry hint suggests a baptism record for a "Mary, daughter of Richard and Mary Harrison, of Blackburn," dated 2 October 1812 — five years before Mary Ellen's own 1817 birth, and therefore not her own baptism. Given this family's well-documented pattern of reusing names after an earlier child's death, this may indicate an older daughter also named Mary who did not survive, whose name was given again to this Mary Ellen. Unconfirmed.
