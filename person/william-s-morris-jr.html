Biography

William S. Morris Jr. was born 21 November 1837 in New Haven, Connecticut — both his death certificate's own age calculation and his obituary agree on this. (The death certificate's birthplace field itself is confusing: it appears to read "Vroman, Pa.," which conflicts with the certificate's own age math and with the independently-sourced obituary; this looks like an informant error rather than a genuine alternate birthplace, but it is noted rather than silently corrected.) He was a son of William Morris Sr. (1811–1890) and Jane Harrison Morris (1818–1885), both already documented on this site, and one of at least seven children.

He married at Vroman and spent much of his working life there as a farmer before retiring; by the time of his death he had moved to Titusville. His 1915 obituary names his wife as "Miss Fannie Morris," but both his 1915 probated will and Ancestry family-tree records give her name as Emily (Amelia) F. Morris (1841–1931) — a conflict between sources left unresolved here. He and his wife had at least five children: Jesse Herbert Morris (1864–1899, already documented on this site, predeceased his father), Harry Allen Morris (1866–1953), Albert William Morris (1874–1958), Gertrude Maude Morris Reynolds (1876–1953), and Mabel Jane Morris (later Myers, 1878–1943).

He died 26 May 1915 at the family home at 525 East Spruce Street in Titusville, after what his obituary describes as a three-year illness of heart disease — matching his death certificate's cause, organic heart disease. He was 77. A prayer service was held at the family home, after which his remains were taken to Harrison Cemetery for burial. He was a member of the Odd Fellows and the Presbyterian church.

He had made his will only sixteen days before he died, on 10 May 1915; it was probated on 10 June 1915. The will left his wife, Emily F. Morris, the use of the residue of his estate for her lifetime, with his daughter Mabel receiving a fixed yearly sum for caring for her mother, and named his surviving children as remainder beneficiaries: son Harry A. (47%), daughter Gertrude Reynolds (30%), a son recorded as "Hebert Morris" (7%), and granddaughter Floy Loud (6%). The bequests as transcribed total only 90 percent of the remaining estate, suggesting the copied excerpt is missing at least one further clause — plausibly a bequest to his son Albert W. Morris, who is not otherwise mentioned in the will as reviewed. The son named "Hebert Morris" is also unresolved: the only son on record with a similar name, Jesse Herbert Morris, had died in 1899, sixteen years before this will was written, so either this refers to a previously undocumented son or reflects some error in the will or its transcription.

Primary source — news notice, The Buffalo News, 27 May 1915

"WILLIAM MORRIS OF TITUSVILLE IS DEAD — TITUSVILLE, Pa., May 27 (Special). — William Morris died last evening at the family home at 525 East Spruce street after an illness of three years of heart disease. He was 77 years old. Mr. Morris was born at New Haven, Conn. He was married to Miss Fannie Morris at Vroman, who survives him with the following children: Harry A. of Sisterville, W. Va.; Albert W. Morris of Vroman, Miss Gertrude Morris of Vroman, and Miss Mabel Morris residing at home. A prayer service will be held at the home Saturday morning at 11 o'clock after which the remains will be taken to the Harrison cemetery for interment. Mr. Morris was an Odd Fellow and a member of the Presbyterian church."

— The Buffalo News, 27 May 1915, p.22
