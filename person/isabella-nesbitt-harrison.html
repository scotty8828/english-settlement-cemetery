Biography

Isabella Nesbitt was born 8 September 1809, likely in Ireland. She was married three times. Her first husband, Alexander Duff, she married 27 March 1828 in New York City; they had one daughter, Elizabeth, born in 1830. Four years into the marriage, Duff died in Elizabeth, New Jersey, leaving Isabella a young widow with a small child.

She remarried in 1834, to John Edmond of New York City, a merchant. The couple lived in New York for about three years, then moved to Pittsburgh for three more, all the while owning 200 acres of land John had purchased in Rome Township. They finally moved to that land themselves, where John began clearing a farm — but on 22 December 1847, he died in a building accident, raising a beam. He and Isabella had six children together: Samuel Mason, John, Mary Jane, Anna, Robert James, and Nancy. Isabella was left a widow a second time, with seven children in all — her oldest son not yet twelve years old — and no relative closer than New York.

She raised her family alone for seven years, working her own new farm in a new country. She married a third time around 1854, to Benjamin Inskip Harrison — her nearest neighbor, and by then also twice-widowed himself. They had one son together, George Matthew Harrison, born in 1855. By the time of Isabella's death, all of her older children from her first two marriages were married and grown; only George, then twenty, remained at home with his aging father.

Isabella was a member of the United Presbyterian Church, having made a public profession of faith at eighteen. In the earliest years of the settlement, she served informally as the neighborhood's physician and nurse — a role remembered specifically in her obituary, written by a neighbor and friend. She died 1 March 1875 of erysipelas, a severe bacterial infection; her illness lasted only a week. She was buried during one of the stormiest days of that winter, and yet, her obituary records, the funeral was the largest ever seen at what the community then called Maple Grove — the cemetery's original name, sited on Benjamin's own farm, and now known as English Settlement Cemetery.

Her monument was carved in the same design as that of Nancy Brown Harrison, Benjamin's first wife, who had died thirty-five years earlier — a quiet way of placing both wives on equal footing in the family's memory.

Obituary — 1875
Published shortly after her death, signed "A Neighbor and Friend."

Died, Monday, March 1st, 1875, Mrs. Isabella Harrison, wife of Benjamin Harrison Sr., of Rome township, aged sixty-five years. Her remains were conveyed to the tomb in the Maple Grove Cemetery, which is on the farm of her husband, and, although the day she was buried was one of the stormiest of this winter, yet there was the largest funeral ever seen in Maple Grove.

Mrs. Harrison came to Rome township thirty-three years ago, from Pittsburgh, Penn'a. She was three times married. Her first husband was Alexander Duff, by whom she had one child. Four years after their marriage, Mr. Duff died in Elizabethtown, New Jersey, leaving her a young widow with one young child. She was next married to Mr. John Edmond of New York City, who was in the mercantile business. They lived some years in New York, then moved to Pittsburgh, and finally to their own land in Rome township, where Mr. Edmond had purchased 200 acres while still living in New York. He met his death by an accident while raising a beam, leaving Isabella a widow with seven children, her oldest son not yet twelve years of age, and no relative nearer than New York.

But being a woman of energy and perseverance, she worked her way along with her family of small children, having the double parental care of them; and their labors were blessed. She kept her children together for seven years, and was again married, to Mr. Benjamin Harrison Sr., her nearest neighbor, by whom she had one son, George M., now twenty years of age, and left alone with his aged father.

Mrs. Harrison was a member of the United Presbyterian Church, having made a public profession of religion at eighteen years of age. She was a pious Christian, charitable and benevolent, kind and affectionate; the beggar was never turned empty away from her door. In the early settlement of Maple Grove, she was physician and nurse for the neighborhood around her, and has left many friends to mourn her loss. Her disease was erysipelas, which baffled all means of relief; her illness was sudden and severe, lasting but one week. She bore her suffering with patience and faith and hope of glorious immortality beyond the grave.

Field-photographed by Scott Morris. Isabella's full name on Find A Grave is recorded as "Isabella Nesbitt Duff Edmund," reflecting her maiden name followed by the surnames of her first two husbands. Ancestry spells her maiden name "Nesbit," one T. Her exact birthplace is uncertain — Find A Grave states Ireland plainly, Ancestry hedges "Ireland or possibly United States" — shown here as Probable. Her daughter Elizabeth Duff married a Gregory; whether this connects to the Gregory family already documented elsewhere on this site (through Elizabeth Harrison Gregory and her descendants) has not been confirmed. This obituary is also the source for this project's discovery that the cemetery's original name was Maple Grove — worth noting on the site more broadly, beyond this page alone.
