Biography

Anna Morris was born 10 September 1879 in Rome Township, the third of the seven children of Inskip Morris (1849–1930) and Mary Ellen Shaw Morris (1853–1922). Her parents had married in October 1873; she had an older sister, Lucy, and an older brother, Warren, and four younger brothers followed, the last of them Inskip Jr., who died at seven when Anna was 22. The family lived near Vrooman.

In late July 1897, P. J. Dailey, 22, applied to the Orphans' Court of Crawford County for a license to marry "Miss Anna Morris" of Rome, who was 17. Because she was a minor, the docket records the consent of her father, Inskip Morris. The couple were married at Titusville on 5 August 1897, and the officiant's certificate was filed the next day. He was Patrick James Dailey (1875–1947). Her obituary, written 57 years later, gives the year as 1896 and the place as Tyronville, so it is off on both counts; Tyronville may have been his home. Four children followed, with dates as shown on Find A Grave: Martin Carl (1898), Lloyd K. (1900), Laura (1901) and Neva (1904). Martin's own marriage record, from Jamestown, New York, in 1917, names his parents as Patrick J. Dailey and Anna Morris and gives his birthplace as Spartansburg, Pennsylvania.

The rest of her middle years are known mostly from family tradition, and need to be read with care. A note attached to Anna's profile in a user-submitted Ancestry family tree says that she divorced Patrick when Neva, the fourth child, was three months old; that Neva was raised by Anna's parents, Inskip and Mary Ellen; that Anna then married her first cousin Reuben Morris (1875–1917) and had twin sons, Harold and Gerald; and that after Reuben died she remarried Patrick Dailey. The note gives no sources, and no divorce record has yet been found. One document may bear on it: the New York State marriage index for 1906 lists an "Anna" among the Morrises married at Jamestown on 28 November 1906 (certificate no. 28030). The page viewed does not show the spouse's name, so it is not yet confirmed to be her, or to be a marriage to Reuben. The twins are shown as both born in 1907. Reuben died 14 September 1917. Anna's mother's obituary in 1922 calls her "Mrs. Anna Morris of Meadville," which fits a widow still using Reuben's name; the name Dailey appears in the records only later.

She outlived four of her six children. Harold Inskip Morris (1907–1937), Gerald "Gerry" Morris (1907–1944), Martin Carl Dailey (1898–1952) and Lloyd K. Dailey (1900–1953) all died before her, the last two within two years of her own death. Her daughters Laura (Mrs. Clark, 1901–1988) and Neva (Mrs. Delmar Williams, 1904–1986) survived her.

By 1954 Anna was living at Evansville, in Rock County, Wisconsin, and for her last six years she made her home with Laura. She had come back to Pennsylvania in June to visit Neva near Titusville. On 5 October 1954 she fell at home and broke her left thigh bone. She was taken to Meadville City Hospital, where she died 18 days later, at 75. Her death certificate lists coronary occlusion, diabetes of about five years' standing, and the fracture; the handwriting is hard to read, and the exact order of causes has not been fully transcribed. Neva, who signed as informant, gave her mother's parents correctly as Inskip Morris and Mary Eller Shaw, and her marital status as widowed.

The funeral was held on Tuesday, 26 October 1954, at Bethel Methodist Church in Titusville, and she was buried that day in the cemetery near Vrooman, in the same ground as her parents, her brother Inskip Jr., and her first-cousin husband Reuben. Her obituary appeared in the Janesville Weekly Gazette, in Wisconsin, on 29 October:

Janesville Weekly Gazette · Janesville, Wisconsin · 29 October 1954 · p. 2
"Mrs. Anna Dailey — EVANSVILLE — Mrs. Anna M. Dailey, 75, Evansville, died Oct. 23 in Meadville City Hospital, Pa. She had been visiting a daughter, Mrs. Delmar Williams, Titusville, Pa., since June. She was born in Rome township, Pa., Sept. 10, 1879, and was married to Patrick Dailey, Tyronville, in 1896. Survivors are two daughters, Mrs. Laura Clark, Evansville, with whom she had resided for six years, and Mrs. Williams; 21 grandchildren; 26 great-grandchildren; and four brothers. Four sons preceded her in death. Funeral services were held Tuesday in Bethel Methodist Church, Titusville, and burial was in Vrooman Cemetery near there."
Transcribed from a newspaper clipping image on Newspapers.com; a few words at the edges of the column are small, so the wording should be checked against the original page.

Death date, 22 or 23 October: The obituary, Find A Grave and the master record give 23 October 1954. The date of death printed in item 4 of the death certificate reads 22 October, though the physician signed on 23 October. The 23rd is used here until the original is checked again. The age of 75 is correct either way.
Her names: Anna appears as Anna Morris (birth name, and in 1922 as "Mrs. Anna Morris of Meadville"), Anna M. Dailey (1954), and Anna Morris Dailey (her stone). The 1930 obituary of her father lists "Mrs. Anna Olson of Racine, Wis." That surname does not match anything else found, and it has not been explained. It may be a third marriage, a second name for another daughter, or a printing error.
Marriage to Patrick Dailey, 1897 not 1896: The Crawford County license docket gives July 1897 for the application and 5 August 1897 for the marriage, at Titusville. Her ages on it agree with a September 1879 birth: 17 on 10 September 1896, and Patrick 22 on 11 February 1897. The docket is handwritten; the day of the application, the groom's township and the officiant's name (read as D. F. Burley) are hard to read and have not been confirmed.
Marriage to Reuben and the later marriage to Patrick: These come only from the Ancestry family-tree note and are unverified. The 1906 New York index entry for an "Anna" Morris at Jamestown may be the marriage to Reuben, but the spouse's name has not been seen. If it is hers, Harold's birth on 2 February 1907 (master record) would follow by only about nine weeks, so the dates need checking against the actual certificate. Jamestown is the nearest New York city to northern Crawford County, and Martin was married there in 1917, so the family evidently used it. Patrick Dailey's place of burial has not been checked.
Martin's age: His 1917 marriage record says he was 21, which would put his birth around 1896, before his parents' license. Find A Grave gives 1898, and the 1897 marriage fits that better. Ages on marriage records are often wrong, so his birth year is left as Find A Grave gives it.
Name of the cemetery: The death certificate and the obituary both say "Vrooman" or "Vroman" cemetery, the name also used in Inskip Morris's 1930 obituary. Find A Grave lists the same ground as Harrison Cemetery, and local newspapers of the time also used Maple Grove. The museum treats these as names for one burial ground.
Cause of death: Read from handwriting on a photographed form, so the wording and the interval entries (such as "18 days") should be rechecked against a clearer copy.
