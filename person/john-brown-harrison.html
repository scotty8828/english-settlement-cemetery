Biography

John Brown Harrison was born in England on 2 April 1824, the son of Benjamin Inskip Harrison and Nancy Brown Harrison. His father was one of the three original landowners named in the 5 April 1834 deed for the Rome Township settlement — already documented at length on this site — making John a member of the second generation of the founding Harrison family, born before his parents ever set foot in America.

The family's emigration and settlement can be traced with unusual precision through the birthdates of John's siblings. His sister Elizabeth was still born in England in March 1826, so the family had not yet crossed by that date. By March 1829 another brother, Thomas, was born in Paterson, Passaic County, New Jersey — placing the family's emigration sometime in those three years. They remained in New Jersey through at least December 1830, when sister Sarah was born there. By May 1836, when sister Ellen was born, the family was in Rome Township, Crawford County, Pennsylvania. John's own 1905 obituary independently confirms this same timeline in his own words: the family came to Paterson in 1826, and moved to Rome Township in 1836 — matching the birthplaces of his siblings almost exactly. John would have been about twelve years old at the family's arrival in Rome Township.

John spent the rest of his life on that same farm. He married his first cousin, Ellen Harrison — documented independently through the 1930 death certificate of their son, Richard, which records his mother's maiden name as Ellen Harrison. John's father, Benjamin Inskip Harrison, and Ellen's father, Richard Harrison (1786–1867), were brothers, both sons of Benjamin Harrison (1759–1824) and Jane Inskip Harrison, already documented at length on this site. John and Ellen were far from the only cousin marriage in this generation of the family: Ellen's sister Margaret married James Morris, William Morris Sr.'s brother, and Ellen's brother William Harrison married a Sarah Harrison of his own. John and Ellen's first child, Richard, was born in February 1848, placing their marriage at approximately 1847 or earlier. A daughter, Eleanora, followed in 1849.

Later in life John suffered a lasting hardship: his obituary records that "for a number of years" he was totally blind. He farmed the family land regardless, remaining on the same ground his parents had settled in 1836 for the rest of his life. He died at home in Rome Township on 12 December 1905, in his eighty-second year, and was remembered in his obituary as "an honest, industrious man, respected by all who knew him." His funeral was held at the United Presbyterian church. He was survived by his wife Ellen, his son Richard, his daughter Eleanora Morris, and several siblings named in his obituary — a fuller record of those family connections follows below.

Obituary
Newspaper obituary, December 1905 (publication not yet identified) · clipping via Find A Grave, added by 4ever a Young
"Mr. John Harrison died at his home at this place Tuesday, December 12th, 1905. He was in his 82nd year. Mr. Harrison was born in England in 1824. He came with his parents to Paterson, N.J., in 1826, and in 1836 moved to this place, where he has lived on the same farm ever since. For a number of years Mr. Harrison suffered the disadvantage of being totally blind. He is survived by an aged wife and one son, Richard, and one daughter, Mrs. Eleanora Morris. He is also survived by four brothers, Thomas, Richard and Edward, of this place, and George, of Oil City, and one sister, Mrs. Edward Morris, of this place. He was an honest, industrious man, respected by all who knew him. Funeral was held at the United Presbyterian church."
The final line of this obituary clipping is faint and only partly legible; the sentence following "United Presbyterian church" has been omitted here rather than guessed at. The full clipping is on file and can be re-examined if a clearer copy becomes available. The exact newspaper this ran in has also not yet been identified.

Family settlement chronology, unusually well-documented: The birthplaces of John's siblings — Elizabeth in England (Mar 1826), Thomas in Paterson, NJ (Mar 1829), Sarah in New Jersey (Dec 1830), Ellen in Rome Township, PA (May 1836) — independently corroborate his own obituary's account of the family's move (Paterson in 1826, Rome Township in 1836). This is one of the more precisely dated family migrations documented on this site.
Two Ellen Harrisons in this family — do not conflate: John's wife, Ellen Harrison Harrison (1828–1911), and John's own sister, Ellen Harrison, later Ellen Harrison Morris (1836–1906), shared the same maiden name and given name but are two different women.
Cousin marriage confirmed: John and Ellen were first cousins. John's father, Benjamin Inskip Harrison, and Ellen's father, Richard Harrison (1786–1867), were brothers — both sons of Benjamin Harrison (1759–1824) and Jane Inskip Harrison. This is one of several cousin marriages found in this generation of the family, alongside Ellen's sister Margaret's marriage to James Morris and Ellen's brother William Harrison's marriage to a Sarah Harrison — part of the pattern of intermarriage this site's broader research is tracking across the Harrison, Morris, and related families.
Obituary's surviving siblings, partly unconfirmed: The 1905 obituary names surviving brothers Thomas, Richard, Edward, and George, and a surviving sister, "Mrs. Edward Morris." Richard is confirmed as Richard Christopher Harrison (1838–1911). "Mrs. Edward Morris" is almost certainly John's sister Ellen, whose married name is documented elsewhere on this site as Ellen Harrison Morris — an Edward Morris (1831–1912) is separately documented in the cemetery master record, consistent with this reading, though not yet directly confirmed as her husband. "Edward" and "George" have not yet been matched with confidence to specific individuals in the master record and are not linked above.
