Biography

Benjamin was born 12 January 1815 in London, England, and christened that August at Saint Andrew Undershaft — a church in the City of London better known as St Mary Axe, one of the few churches to survive both the Great Fire of London and the Blitz. He was the second of five children born to Benjamin Wheattall Sr. (c.1783–1858), a London merchant, and Iphigenia Dodd (c.1786–1851). Two of his siblings never reached adulthood: an older sister, also named Iphegenia, died in London as an infant, and his brother William Edward died there at fourteen. Only Benjamin, his brother Henry, and his sister Phoebe grew up. Phoebe would go on to marry into the Delaforce family, prominent Port wine merchants, and spend her life in Oporto, Portugal — a connection that appears to have gotten tangled, a generation or two later, into family memory of the Wheatalls themselves being "Portuguese."

Benjamin and Henry were bound out to a ship's master as boys and became sailors, crossing the Atlantic together many times over. Family memory, recorded by Benjamin's own grandnephew Robert C. Wheatall, holds that the brothers made a trip to Rome Township specifically to visit an old sailor they knew named Stucky. While they were there, their ship sailed without them — twice, if the fuller family account is believed, once outbound and once on their return trip to catch up with it. With no ship left to chase, they stayed.

Henry went on to marry Elizabeth Harrison (1823–1883), daughter of Richard Harrison, on Christmas Day 1845 — just over three months after Benjamin's death. Benjamin himself never married, but family memory holds that he was courting Elizabeth's cousin, Betsy Harrison (later Elizabeth Harrison Gilson, 1826–1904, already documented on this site) — daughter of Richard's brother Benjamin Inskip Harrison — in the weeks before he died. He had called on the Harrisons one evening to see her and stayed the night. He was found dead in bed the next morning.

On 18 July 1845, less than two months before his death, Benjamin had purchased 158 acres and 139 perches of land in Oil Creek Township from Patrick Sloan for three hundred and sixty dollars, paid in gold and silver. He died 15 September 1845, without a will, at thirty years, nine months, and three days old. A coroner's inquest — later transcribed from the original Crawford County record — ruled, in the language of the time, that he had died "by the visitation of God in a natural way and not otherwise": an unexplained but non-suspicious death. His brother Henry was granted Letters of Administration over his estate that November, with a $200 bond and an estate inventory valued at $110.77. Benjamin Harrison and B.M. Gilson — the family Betsy would later marry into — stood as witnesses.

Benjamin was buried at what is now English Settlement Cemetery. His monument survives, its epitaph a sailor's verse: "How sweet to stand when tempests tear the main, on the firm cliff and mark the seaman's toil — not that another's danger sooths the soul, but from such toil how sweet to feel secure."

Bob Wheatall's compiled research resolves nearly everything that was previously uncertain on this page: Benjamin's exact London birthplace and parents are now documented rather than inferred from Ancestry hints, and the family's "Portuguese" origin appears to trace to his sister Phoebe's marriage into the Delaforce Port-wine family of Oporto — not to the Wheatalls themselves. One detail from the earlier family letter doesn't hold up: Raymond Foley's 1989 account named the ship as the "Great Eastern," but that ship wasn't built until 1858, thirteen years after Benjamin's death — the ship's actual name is not otherwise recorded. Henry and Elizabeth's full family — his three marriages, Effie's marriage into the Morris family, the rest of their descendants — is substantial enough that it's being kept on a dedicated Wheatall family page rather than folded in here, since none of them are buried at English Settlement Cemetery.
