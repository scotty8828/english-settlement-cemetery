Biography

Dorcy A. Reynolds was born 1 November 1872 in Pennsylvania, a son of Henry Lowery Reynolds (1846–1877) and Hannah Slater (1854–1874). Both of his parents died while he was still a young child — his mother in 1874, when he was not yet two, and his father in 1877, when he was five — leaving him orphaned by early childhood. He had a half-sister, Mrs. Eddie Conrad, who by 1922 was living in California; no further record of her has been found.

He married Gertrude Maude Morris (1876–1953), already documented on this site. They had two children: Bessie L. Reynolds, who died in infancy in 1892, and William Henry Reynolds (1893–1971). Dorcy lived near Shelmadine Springs before moving to the Vroman area, where he farmed for thirty years and was, per his obituary, "held in high esteem by a wide circle of friends." He belonged to Sparta Grange, Queen City Lodge I.O.O.F., and the Titusville Lodge of the Fraternal Order of Eagles.

On 2 October 1922, while helping with the grain harvest on his farm, Dorcy was seriously injured in a threshing accident: after the machine was shut down for the midday meal, he jumped down from a staging erected near the thresher and landed on the handle of a pitchfork concealed in a nearby pile of straw. The injury was severe, and he was taken to the Titusville City Hospital, where surgeons operated in an effort to save him. His death certificate also lists lobar pneumonia, of about a month's standing, as a contributing condition. Complications set in, and he died at the hospital just before midnight on 4 October 1922, two days after the accident. He was buried three days later at what his certificate records as "Vrooman Cemetery."

Primary source — Titusville Herald, 5 October 1922

"DORCEY REYNOLDS DIED JUST BEFORE MIDNIGHT — Well Known Farmer, Residing Near Vrooman, Fatally Injured on Monday — Dorcey Reynolds, mention of whom was made in the Herald yesterday as having been seriously injured at his home near Vrooman on Monday, passed away at 11:30 o'clock last evening at the City Hospital. The body was removed to the undertaking rooms of Charles Courtney, Jr. to be prepared for burial and arrangements for the funeral will be made later. As stated in the Herald yesterday, Mr. Reynolds was injured at his farm home shortly after noon on Monday. He had been assisting in the work of threshing the years harvest of grain on the farm, and soon after the threshing machine was shut down for dinner Mr. Reynolds, who had been working on a staging erected near the thresher, jumped to the ground. There was a pile of straw near the staging and in it was concealed a pitchfork. Mr. Reynolds struck on the handle of the fork, which protruded through the rectum and punctured the bladder, besides causing other internal injuries. The injured man was hurried to the Titusville City Hospital, where an operation was performed by surgeons in the hope of saving his life. Complications developed, however, and he passed away at the hour stated.

Mr. Reynolds was born on Nov. 1, 1872 so was nearly fifty years of age. He had resided in the vicinity of Vrooman for thirty years and prior to that time lived near Shelmadine Springs. He is survived by his wife and one son, William. He also leaves a half-sister, Mrs. Eddie Conrad, who resides in California. Mr. Reynolds was held in high esteem by a wide circle of friends and his untimely death will be a matter of much regret. He was a member of Sparta Grange, of Queen City Lodge I.O.O.F., and the Titusville Lodge, Fraternal Order of Eagles."

— Titusville Herald, 5 October 1922, as reproduced on Find A Grave
