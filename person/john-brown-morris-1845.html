Biography

John Brown Morris was born 14 September 1855 in Crawford County, a son of William "Billie" Morris Sr. (1811–1890) and Jane Harrison Morris (1818–1885), both already documented on this site. He had at least five siblings: William S. Morris Jr. (1837–1915), Sarah Ann Morris Myers (1839–1906), Nancy Elizabeth Morris Magee (1844–1874), Mary Jane Morris Sedden (1846–1930), James L. "Jimmy" Morris (1848–1921), and Benjamin Harrison Morris (1852–1901) — all but Sarah and Nancy already documented on this site.

He was a farmer near Buell, in Rome Township, and lived there most of his life. He died 5 February 1926 after an illness of about two months, which one obituary attributes to Bright's disease — a period term for chronic kidney disease. His funeral was held at the family home, with Rev. Fred Smith of Centerville, pastor of the United Brethren church, officiating; the pallbearers were neighbors and friends Frank Henton, Roy Mitchell, Charles Farrell, and William Morgan. He was buried at what his obituaries record as "Vrooman" or "Vrooman cemetery" — this site's own Harrison Cemetery. One obituary summed him up simply: "a good citizen, a kind neighbor and friend, husband and father."

His wife — Seraph Matilda Chase Morris
27 October 1861 – 31 August 1926

Seraph was born in Rome Township, a daughter of James Henry Chase (1837–1921) and Nancy A. Fish Chase (1837–1909) — a separate Fish family line from Saraph Fish Morris, already documented on this site, who was John Morris's first wife. She and John Brown Morris had one daughter, Zelma (1878–1964), whose married name appears in the records as both "Boles" and "Peterson" — possibly two successive marriages, not resolved here.

Seraph outlived John by just under seven months, dying 31 August 1926 at their home at Buell Corners after a three-year illness. Unlike her husband, she was not buried at Harrison Cemetery — her own burial was at what her obituary calls the "Mageetown cemetery," formally Immaculate Conception Cemetery in Centerville, Crawford County. She does not have a separate page on this site; her information is recorded here alongside her husband's.

Primary sources — his obituaries, February 1926

"Centerville, Pa. — The funeral of John B. Morris was held Monday afternoon at 2:00 o'clock from the family residence, and interment was made in Harrison Cemetery, at Vrooman. Mr. Morris died Friday evening after an illness of about two months, aged 71 years. The widow, Mrs. Seraph Morris, and one daughter, Mrs. Zelma Boles, survive." (The Erie Times, 11 Feb 1926)

"Funeral services in memory of John B. Morris were conducted from the family home near Buell in Rome township, Rev. Fred Smith of Centerville, pastor of the United Brethren church officiating, the minister speaking words of comfort to the bereaved family. The interment was made in the church cemetery at Vrooman, the pallbearers being neighbors and friends, Frank Henton, Roy Mitchell, Charles Farrell and William Morgan. Mr. Morris is survived by his wife and one daughter, Mrs. Zelma Boles, who has been faithful and untiring in her devotion throughout the long illness of her father. Mr. Morris was a good neighbor and friend and will be greatly missed in the neighborhood where he resided so long." (Titusville Herald, 12 Feb 1926)

"The demise of John B. Morris, one of the best-known citizens of Rome township, while death was not unexpected, came nevertheless as a shock to his numerous friends. Mr. Morris had passed his 'three score and ten years,' death being the result of 'Bright's disease.'" (Titusville Herald, 11 Feb 1926, p.7)

— as reproduced on Find A Grave. Note: the Erie Times gives his age as 71; his own documented birth and death dates work out to 70 years, 4 months, 22 days, matching Find A Grave's header — treated here as the more reliable figure.

"Mrs. Seraph M. Morris, aged 64 years, 10 months and three days, died at her home at Buell Corners, yesterday afternoon at 12:30 o'clock after an illness of three years. She is survived by one daughter, Zelma Boles, who resides at home. Funeral services in her memory will be held from the Mageetown church on Thursday morning at 9 o'clock. Interment will be made in the Mageetown cemetery."

Long-standing confusion with a cousin, now resolved. John Brown Morris (1855–1926) was for some time difficult to distinguish from a separately-documented John Morris of the same generation, since both men married women who went by "Seraph"/"Saraph" — this John's wife was Seraph Matilda Chase (1861–1926), daughter of Nancy A. Fish Chase; the other man's wife was Saraph Fish Morris (already documented on this site), daughter of Ira W. Fish. Census records for 1860, 1870, 1880, 1900, and 1910 — the last showing John and Seraph living in Crawford County, Illinois during the local oil boom, with a Guy Goodrich boarding with them — confirmed the two men as entirely separate people with separate wives, both coincidentally connected to different Fish family lines.
Daughter's married name. Zelma appears as "Zelma Boles" in both her parents' 1926 obituaries, but as "Zelma Morris Peterson" in family tree records — possibly two successive marriages, not resolved here.
