Biography

Nancy Brown was born in England in 1794. On 1 January 1818, she married Benjamin Inskip Harrison (1797–1875), son of Benjamin and Jane Inskip Harrison — meaning the marriage took place nearly a decade before the wider Harrison family's 1827 emigration to America.

Nancy was one of the three women of the founding journey into the Rome Township wilderness in 1833 — alongside her mother-in-law Jane Inskip Harrison and her sister-in-law Jane Harrison Morris — as her husband Benjamin, his brother Inskip Harrison, and brother-in-law Richard Morris carved out what became the English Settlement. Together, Nancy and Benjamin had nine children: Jane, Benjamin, John, Elizabeth, Thomas, Sarah, Ellen, Richard, and Edward, born between 1818 and 1840.

Nancy died on 9 September 1840 in Crawford County, Pennsylvania, at approximately 45 or 46 years old — just days after the birth of her youngest child, Edward, suggesting she likely died of childbirth complications, the leading cause of death for women of her age in this era. She was buried at what is now English Settlement Cemetery, among the earliest recorded burials at the site. Benjamin Inskip Harrison remarried some years later, to Isabella Nesbitt (1809–1875), a twice-widowed Irish immigrant whose own family had already settled land in Rome Township; they had one son together.

Nancy's own parents, place of birth in England, and the circumstances of her death are not yet documented. If you have information, please use the contribution form below.

Sources & notes

  • English Settlement Cemetery master record (burial #001)
  • Ancestry.com hint suggestion: William Brown & Mary Rogerson Brown as potential parents (unconfirmed)
  • "Inskip Morris Travel Diary" — cited on Ancestry as source for William Brown's death date/age
  • Research by Scott Morris
Nancy's daughter Jane Harrison Morris (1818–1885) is a different person from Richard Morris's wife, also named Jane Harrison Morris (1806–1887) — this resolves a long-standing ambiguity between two similarly-named records in the cemetery data. Her son Edward Inskip Harrison (1840–1914) is currently described on Jane Inskip Harrison's page as the son of Inskip Harrison, but appears from this record to actually be Benjamin and Nancy's son — that page needs correcting. A working theory: with Nancy's death likely from childbirth complications just days after Edward's birth, his uncle Inskip Harrison's family — living nearby in a still-isolated settlement — may have raised the infant. If Edward appeared in Inskip's household in a later census, that household listing was likely misread as parentage by Ancestry trees, which would explain how the error spread. This is a theory, not yet confirmed. Nancy's parents are listed as William Brown and Mary Rogerson Brown of Lancashire, England, per Ancestry hint suggestions from two separate user-submitted family trees — not independently verified, and shown here so a visitor with better documentation can confirm or correct it. Several other children (Thomas, Ellen, Richard) have not yet been matched to cemetery records. Benjamin Inskip Harrison's second wife, Isabella Nesbitt Harrison (1809–1875), is confirmed via Find A Grave — she was previously married to Alexander Duff and John Edmund, both of whose families are already documented in the cemetery data (Elizabeth Duff Gregory, Samuel Mason Edmond, John Edmond). Her Find A Grave memorial also confirms the cemetery was originally called Maple Grove Cemetery, independently corroborating a name previously found only in a family memoir.
