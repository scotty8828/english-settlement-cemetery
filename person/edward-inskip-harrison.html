Biography

Edward Inskip Harrison was born 3 September 1840 in Pennsylvania, the youngest child of Benjamin Inskip Harrison and Nancy Brown Harrison, both already documented on this site. His mother died just six days after his birth, on 9 September 1840. His father later remarried, and Edward had at least one younger half-brother from that second marriage, George Matthew Harrison (1855–1926), also documented on this site. Edward's known full siblings included Jane Harrison Morris, Benjamin Harrison, John Brown Harrison, Elizabeth "Betsy" Harrison Gilson, Sarah Harrison Harrison, Ellen Harrison Morris, and Richard Christopher Harrison, all documented on this site.

During the Civil War, Edward enlisted 2 September 1861 at Titusville, in Company K of the 57th Pennsylvania Infantry, and was mustered in as sergeant on 15 November 1861 at Harrisburg. A military service card describes him at enlistment as 21 years old, 5 feet 10 inches tall, with a fair complexion, blue eyes, and black hair; his occupation was given as farmer. He was discharged on a surgeon's certificate — a medical discharge — though the exact date is not recorded. A separate veterans' grave-registration card, compiled decades later, lists his rank at discharge as private rather than sergeant; this discrepancy in rank is not resolved.

Some years after the war, Edward married Amelia E. Rigby (1849–1945), already documented on this site, a native of New York state. They had at least three children: Irene Ellen Harrison (1873–1958), Arthur Emanuel Harrison (1879–1966), and Raymond Sumner Harrison (1885–1955), all documented on this site. Edward was a member of the United Presbyterian church at Vrooman and of the Grange.

Edward Inskip Harrison died 10 January 1914 at the family home near Vrooman, after four years of declining health, at the age of seventy-three. His funeral, originally set for the following day, was postponed until Thursday when a blizzard blockaded the roads; delivering a casket to the family home reportedly took the funeral director twice the usual time and was hard on the horses. Services were held at the family home and at the Vrooman United Presbyterian church, led by Rev. Samuel Sample, with music including the 25th and 143rd Psalms and "Rock of Ages." He was buried in the Vrooman cemetery — confirmed both by his own 1914 obituary and, decades later, by a Pennsylvania veterans' grave-registration card recording his place of interment as English Settlement.

Obituary
Newspaper obituary, January 1914 (publication not yet identified) · initial death notice
"Edward J. Harrison, a life long resident of Rome township, a veteran of the Civil war and widely known and honored throughout this section, died at the family home near Vrooman Saturday afternoon, after suffering for four years from a complication of ailments. The deceased, who was about 73 years old, was born and raised in Rome township where, many years ago, he was married to Miss Amelia Rugby of New York state who, with there children, all living at home survives. The children are Irene, Arthur and Raymond Harrison. The deceased is also survived by one brother, George Harrison of Rome Township. He was a member of the United Presbyterian church congregation at Vrooman and of the Grange. Funeral services will be conducted tomorrow afternoon at 1 o'clock at the residence, and at 2 o'clock at the U.P. church at Vrooman."
Newspaper notice, January 1914 · "Funeral Postponed"
"The funeral of Edward J. Harrison of Rome Township, who died at the family home on Saturday, instead of being held today as was at first announced, has been postponed until Thursday. The change was brought about by the blizzard which has so blockaded the roads that it would be impossible to get over them today, with carriages or the funeral car. Funeral Director Charles Courtney, Jr., yesterday took a casket to the bereaved home, and the trip was a terrible one, the driver and Mr. Courtney being obliged to face the blizzard and contend with the snow-drifted highways, the ride taking twice as long as under ordinary circumstances, and being very hard on the horses."
Newspaper report, January 1914 · "Many Were Present at Funeral Thursday — Services for E. J. Harrison at Vrooman"
"Funeral services for Edward J. Harrison of Rome Township, who died last Saturday at the family home near Vrooman, which had been postponed from Tuesday on account of the blizzard, were conducted yesterday afternoon. There was present a large assemblage of mourning friends, neighbors and relatives. A very pretty contribution of flowers was in evidence. The services, which were conducted by Rev. Samuel Sample of the First Presbyterian church of this city, began at 1 o'clock at the family home, when prayer service was conducted, and at the Vrooman United Presbyterian church at 2 o'clock. At the church Rev. Mr. Sample preached a sermon. He was assisted by a choir made up of Messrs. G. G. Conover, S. W. Stewart, Mrs. Ida Kirk and Mrs. Edith Harrison, who sang selections from the Twenty fifth and 143rd Psalms, and "Rock of Ages." Miss Mildred Conover presided at the organ. Burial was in the Vrooman cemetery and Messrs. William Gilson, B. J. Richard, Samuel, S. W., E. R. and E. R. Harrison acted as pallbearers."

Rank discrepancy between two military records: His muster card records him as mustered in as sergeant; a later veterans' grave-registration card lists his rank simply as private. Not resolved — he may have held both ranks at different points, or one record may be in error.
Grave registration card shows conflicting burial locations, later corrected: The Pennsylvania veterans' grave-registration card lists three possible cemetery names — Rose Hill, Red Oak, and English Settlement — recorded across entries from 1935 to 1947, the last marked "Correction." This is read as an administrative record-keeping process resolving to English Settlement as his actual place of burial, consistent with his own 1914 obituary's mention of "the Vrooman cemetery" and this site's own records.
Possible Conover and Gilson connections at the funeral: Musicians at his funeral included G. G. Conover and Miss Mildred Conover, and pallbearers included a William Gilson — both surnames already connected to this site through other families (the Conovers via Martha Melvina Conover Gregory, the Gilsons via Elizabeth "Betsy" Harrison Gilson). No specific relationship between these individuals and Edward's own family has been established; noted here as a possible thread for future research.
