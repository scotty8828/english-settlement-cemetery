Biography

Richard Harrison was born 13 January 1785 in Blackburn, Lancashire, England, the son of Benjamin Harrison and Jane Inskip Harrison — the couple whose emigration a generation later would help found the English Settlement. He grew up alongside at least four siblings: Elizabeth, Benjamin Inskip, Jane, and Edward Harrison, with a younger brother, Inskip Harrison, born when Richard was already 27. His father Benjamin died in 1824; his mother Jane outlived him by two decades, dying in Rome Township in 1845 as one of the earliest burials at what would become English Settlement Cemetery.

Richard married Mary Ann Malley, though no record of the date has yet been found. Together they raised at least six children: Margaret (who married a Morris), Jane (who married Eliab Sedden), Mary Ellen (who married James Vroman), William, Elizabeth (who married Henry Wheatall), and Ellen — the youngest, born in Blackburn as late as 1828. That birthplace suggests Richard's own household may have remained in Lancashire, and then in New Jersey, well after his mother Jane Inskip Harrison and other siblings had already begun settling Rome Township — his daughter Jane's own family doesn't appear in Crawford County until sometime after 1840. Richard and his household may have joined the rest of the family in Pennsylvania later than the settlement's earliest pioneers.

A family letter, written by Richard's sister Jane to her husband Richard Morris, gives a rare, lively glimpse of the Harrison family at home in Rome Township — describing a Christmas Day double wedding for two of Richard's daughters, the whole family turning out in sleighs, and the small talk of a rural Pennsylvania congregation. It is quoted in full below.

Richard died 8 October 1867 in Rome Township, Crawford County, and was buried at English Settlement Cemetery beside Mary Ann and generations of the family they raised.

A family letter — Richard's sister Jane, writing home
Written by Jane (Harrison) Morris to her husband Richard Morris, describing a Christmas Day wedding for two of her brother Richard's daughters. Exact date not yet established.

On Christmas Day I went to the wedding of brother Richard's 2 daughters, Mary to James Vroman and Elizabeth to Henry Wheatall, who bought the place your father used to live on. They were married by the Presbyterian minister of Titusville. There was a large company of us, chiefly relations.

Me and Edward was at the Vroman's the next day at the infare. Inskip and Billy Harrison called in today as they were going to the meeting. Joseph Barnsdale preached the sermon, and Mr. Post, a Mormon, another — the Mormons are next to the Universalists in liberality of mind. Brother Richard and Molly, Margaret and her baby, and the Vroman's were all at the meeting today. They came in their sleighs.

— from Jane's letter to her husband, Richard Morris
Transcribed and shared on Find A Grave by contributor Debra Orris, 2023. "Molly" appears to be a family nickname for Mary Ann (Malley) Harrison, Richard's wife; "Margaret and her baby" is likely Richard's daughter Margaret Harrison Morris.

Birth year is given as 1785 on Find A Grave and 1786 on Ancestry — both agree on 13 January, so this is likely a one-year transcription slip somewhere along the line rather than a real dispute; shown here as Probable pending a primary record.
Richard's own household may have settled in Rome Township later than the rest of the family. All of Richard's children are recorded as born in England as late as 1828 (Ellen, in Blackburn), and his daughter Jane's own family — William Sedden born 1834 in Paterson, NJ, another daughter born 1840 in Essex County, NJ — appears to have remained in New Jersey through at least 1840, well after Jane Inskip Harrison and other family members had already begun settling Rome Township. This suggests Richard and his household may have stayed behind, possibly in New Jersey, for a period after his mother, brother, and sister pioneered the settlement, joining them in Crawford County only later. This is an inference from birthplace and timing, not a confirmed migration record — flagged as Probable, worth testing against census records if any turn up.
Correction applied: Jane Harrison Sedden (1810–1855) is Richard Harrison's own daughter, making her a granddaughter of Jane Inskip Harrison, not her direct daughter. Jane Inskip Harrison's actual daughter is a different person, Jane Harrison Morris (1806–1887, wife of Richard Morris) — the family reused the name "Jane Harrison" across two generations. Jane Harrison Sedden's own page and William Sedden's page have been corrected to reflect this.
