Biography

Ann was born around 1790 in England, most likely in Lancashire, the county her husband James Morris called home. She has long been recorded here as "Ann Azlean Morris," though the origin of "Azlean" is unclear. A handwritten family memoir shared with the museum by researcher Bob Wheatall names her "Anna (Aglen) Morris," raising the possibility that "Azlean" is a mishearing or miscopying of "Aglen." The name Azlean is kept here as the recorded name pending further confirmation.

Ann married James Morris, though no record of the date or place has yet been found. Together they raised at least four sons who lived to adulthood: Richard (b. 1810), William Sr. (b. 1811), James (b. 1813), and Robert (b. 1814) — all born in Lancashire, England, in the years before the family crossed the Atlantic.

According to the same family memoir, the Morrises landed in New York City in 1826. From there, the family is believed to have settled for a time just across the Hudson River in Paterson, New Jersey, before eventually making the journey west to Rome Township, Crawford County, Pennsylvania, where James and Ann would spend the rest of their lives among the small community of English families who founded the English Settlement.

Ann died on 13 March 1863, five years after James, and was laid to rest beside him at English Settlement Cemetery.

Additional biography will be added as research deepens. If you have information about Ann Azlean Morris — including whether "Azlean" should in fact be "Aglen," and about a possible father Charles Aglen, whose identity remains unconfirmed — please use the contribution form below.
