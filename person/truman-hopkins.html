Biography

Truman Hopkins was born 5 March 1814 in Otsego County, New York. Little is yet documented of his early life or the circumstances that eventually brought him to Rome Township, Pennsylvania.

He married Lydia Ann Sweet (1816–1895), daughter of Thomas Sweet and Lydia Bradt, on 15 November 1837 at the home of her father in Virgil, Cortland County, New York, with Frederick B. Sweet as witness. The couple raised at least three children: Sophronia A. Hopkins Sweet (1842–1917), Charles Hopkins (1843–1905), and Leroy "Roy" Hopkins (1844–1906). Through the 1840s and 1850s the family lived in Cayuga County, New York — first at Locke, then Scipio — before moving south into Pennsylvania, where they were living at Chatham, Tioga County, by 1860. At some point around 1870 Truman spent time in Marion, Michigan, confirmed by family descendants though not yet documented in detail, before the family's path eventually brought them to Crawford County.

At forty-eight years old, Truman enlisted for Civil War service on 14 September 1862, joining Company I of the 150th Pennsylvania Volunteer Infantry as a private — the same regiment his son Charles had joined just over two weeks earlier, on 28 August, and the same company his son-in-law John B. Sweet, husband of daughter Sophronia, also served in. Truman's own service was brief: he was discharged for disability on a surgeon's certificate on 21 November 1862, little more than two months after enlisting, likely owing to his age or health. Charles remained in service considerably longer, later transferring to Battery B of the 45th Pennsylvania Volunteers in December 1863.

Truman Hopkins died 20 February 1891 at seventy-six years old. No cause of death has yet been documented. He was buried at the English Settlement, where his son Leroy is also buried.

Cause of death unknown: Truman died at 76 with no cause yet documented. No obituary has been located.
Monument's bottom line not yet transcribed: The photographed headstone appears to carry an additional worn line below the birth/death dates, possibly denoting a church role — not confidently legible from the available photo. Worth a closer look on a future field visit.
Military service not yet reflected in the master record: Truman's own Civil War service (150th PA Volunteer Infantry, Company I, discharged on surgeon's certificate) is documented here via Ancestry, but the cemetery master record does not currently tag him as a veteran the way it does his son Charles. Worth updating if the master record is revised.
Sophronia's birth year corrected: Find A Grave and this project's earlier notes gave 1841; a descendant-supplied family record gives 31 March 1842. The later, more specific date is used here.
Michigan residence confirmed: The Ancestry-generated life story's claim that Truman lived in Marion, Livingston County, Michigan, around 1870 was initially treated with caution given that story's acknowledged unreliability, but descendant Kelley Johnson has since confirmed directly that he did in fact spend time in Michigan. Exact dates and circumstances are not yet documented.

Descendant contact

Kelley Johnson (Ancestry username johnsonrealtor), a great-great-great-great-granddaughter of Truman Hopkins, responded to an inquiry about the "Truman Hopkins Story.ftms" file attached to his Ancestry profile, and provided a translation of its contents, noting it is an AI-generated Ancestry "life story" with known errors. The specific dates and names it contains — the 1837 marriage record, Lydia Ann Sweet's parents, the children's birth details, and the Company I designation — have been incorporated above where they could be reasonably trusted. Kelley also confirmed the Michigan residence directly from family knowledge, and noted she has since added a hand-written certified letter documenting the 1837 marriage to Truman's Ancestry profile — a document worth reviewing directly for further detail.
