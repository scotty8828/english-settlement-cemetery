Biography

William B. Edmond was born in 1871, the son of Samuel Mason Edmond and Rebecca St. Clair Edmond, of Morris Corners in Rome Township. He was a grandson of Isabella Nesbitt Harrison, the neighborhood's early physician and nurse, and Isabella's second husband John Edmond — making William part of the same Edmond family already documented on Isabella's biography page, and a great-grandson, through her, of the founding generation of the English Settlement.

His father, Samuel, enlisted at Titusville in Company D of the 18th Pennsylvania Cavalry and served through most of the war in the Army of the Potomac. In 1864 he was captured by Mosby's guerrillas near Orange Courthouse, Virginia, and held first at Richmond, then at Belle Isle — one of the Confederacy's harshest prisoner-of-war camps — before eventually being discharged and returning to the family farm in Pennsylvania.

William died 12 February 1878, at six years and five months old; no cause of death is recorded. Sometime after his death, the family left Rome Township for Iowa: his mother Rebecca died on a farm near Vinton, Iowa, on 30 August 1879, roughly a year and a half after her young son's death in Pennsylvania. William's sister Essabelle would die in 1886, at fifteen.

His marble headstone carries a family verse alongside a paraphrase of scripture: "Our Willie's gone before us. O, may we meet again in heaven's glorious mansion, forever remain. Suffer little children to come unto me and forbid them not, for of such is the kingdom of God" — the latter a rendering of Mark 10:14, a passage commonly chosen for children's graves in this era.

William's Find A Grave memorial independently states that this cemetery "was first called Maple Grove" — a second, separate confirmation of the name already established through Isabella Nesbitt Harrison's own 1875 obituary. No cause of death is recorded for William. His memorial gives the specific locale "Morris Corners" within Rome Township, a place name not otherwise documented elsewhere on this site.
Samuel's own obituary names a son, Albert H. Edmond, living in Long Beach at the time of writing and a junior partner in a firm called Baker & Edmond — a sibling not listed on William's Find A Grave memorial, which instead names Millie B. Edmond. Both may be real, separate children, or one record may contain an error; this has not been resolved. The obituary also states Samuel and Rebecca's family "moved to Iowa about twenty-seven years ago" relative to Samuel's own death, which sits awkwardly against Rebecca's 1879 death near Vinton, Iowa — suggesting either an earlier, separate move connected to Rebecca's final illness, or some imprecision in the obituary's own timeline.
