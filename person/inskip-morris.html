Biography

Inskip Morris was born 29 December 1849 in Rome Township, near Vrooman, a son of Richard Morris (1810–1871) and Jane Harrison Morris (1806–1887), both already documented on this site. He was a brother of Benjamin Morris (1840–1915), Pvt. James Morris (1837–1863), Edward Morris (1831–1912), and John Morris (1845–1928), all already documented on this site, as well as William S. Morris (1842–1891), not buried here, who married Eleanora Harrison Morris, sister of Richard B. Harrison, also documented on this site.

On 9 October 1873, Inskip married Mary Ellen Shaw (1853–1922), already documented on this site. Together they had seven children: Lucy Morris Stoke (1874–1951), Warren Inskip Morris (1876–1960), Anna Morris Dailey (1879–1954, already documented on this site — she later married Reuben Morris, also documented here), Clifton Morris (1882–1964), William E. "Will" Morris (1885–1966), David Roy Morris (1890–1964), and a son, Inskip Morris Jr., who died at the age of seven.

Family recollection — Titusville Herald, 12 January 1922

In January 1922, when Inskip was 72, a local paper described him walking nearly thirty miles from his Rome Township home to Centreville "in fine shape," remarking that "it appears that the father of Mr. Morris was 'some walker' in his day, too." As a boy, the article recounts, Inskip watched his father Richard Morris walk to the county seat and back in a single day, arriving home at 10 o'clock at night, and on another occasion carry a heavy iron casting all the way to Meadville to have it repaired. Richard brought Inskip back a rare toy from that trip — a metal spinning top with a string, painted red and white — a gift the boy who owned it was considered "very fortunate" to have.

Widowed after Mary Ellen's death in 1922, Inskip continued farming into his eighties. About two weeks before his own death, he traveled to Kansas and other parts of the West to visit relatives. He fell ill during the trip — his death certificate names Nebraska as where the illness was contracted — and doctors advised him to return home at once. He was unconscious by the time he reached home on a Thursday morning and died about twenty-four hours later, on 11 July 1930, at his home near Vrooman, at the age of 80. His death certificate records the cause as heat exhaustion, with chronic nephritis and senility listed as contributory.

Funeral services were held from the family home in Rome Township on 14 July, Rev. M. M. Clark, pastor of Townville Baptist Church, officiating, with a large attendance of relatives, neighbors, and friends. His grandsons Howard, Edwin, Delmar, William, and Kenneth Morris, together with Lawrence and Theodore Stoke, served as pallbearers. Burial followed at Harrison Cemetery.

Obituaries
Titusville Herald · 12 July 1930, p. 3

ROME TOWNSHIP PIONEER PASSES AWAY YESTERDAY — Inskip Morris, Aged 80 Years, Born And Always Resided in Vicinity Of Vrooman — Inskip Morris, in his 81st year and native of Rome township, passed away at 8 o'clock yesterday at his home in the vicinity of Vrooman, a few miles north of Titusville. Funeral services in his memory will be conducted from the family home, Monday afternoon at 2 o'clock and interment will be made in the Vrooman cemetery. About two weeks ago, Mr Morris went to points in Kansas and other parts of the West for a visit with relatives. He became ill and was advised to return home. He started on the trip and became ill on the train, being unconscious when he arrived on Thursday morning, and passed away peacefully about 24 hours later. Mr Morris was born in Rome township near Vrooman on December 29, 1849, son of Richard and Jane Harrison Morris, who came from England and settled in Rome township. On October 9, 1873, he was united in marriage with Miss Mary Ellen Shaw, who passed away on June 15, 1922. To them were born seven children, six of whom survive as follows: Mrs Lucy Stokes of Titusville, Mrs Anna Olson of Racine, Wis., Warren Morris of Trimms, Clifton Morris of Buells, Willis Morris of Vrooman and David Morris of Kohnson, Kan. A son, Inskip Morris, Jr, died at the age of seven years. Mr Morris enjoyed a wide acquaintance throughout the community where he spent his long life and was held in high esteem.

Titusville Herald · 19 July 1930, p. 2 — "Funerals"

Inskip Morris — Services in memory of Inskip Morris were conducted from the family home in Rome township on July 14, at 2 pm, with Rev M M Clark, pastor of Townville Baptist church, officiating. There was a large attendance of relatives, neighbors and friends and following the service, interment was made in the Vrooman cemetery, the following grandsons acting as pall-bearers: Howard, Edwin, Delmar, William and Kenneth Morris, Lawrence and Theodore Stoke.

Erie Daily Times · 18 July 1930 — "Throng Attends Funeral Service"

Spartansburg, Pa., July 18. — Funeral service for the late Inskip Morris, 81, pioneer Rome township resident, were held at the family home with interment following in the Maple Grove cemetery. They were largely attended, many coming from a distance. Mr. Morris went two weeks ago to points in Kansas and elsewhere in the Middle West to visit relatives. He was taken ill there and was advised by doctors who were called to return home at once. He was unconscious by the time he reached here and his death followed 24 hours later. He is survived by six children: Mrs. Lucy Stokes of Titusville, Mrs. Anna Olson of Racine, Wis.; Warren, of Trimms; Clifton, of Buell's; William, of Vroman and David, of Johnson, Kan.

Monument
A Find A Grave photograph (contributed by Virginia Bates-Hulings) shows a shared upright monument with his wife, inscribed "INSKIP SON OF RICHARD & JANE HARRISON MORRIS, BORN DEC. 28, 1848, DIED JULY 11, 1930, AGED 80 YR 6 MO 13 DA" and, beneath it, "MARY HELEN SHAW HIS WIFE, BORN APR 17, 1853, DIED JUNE 15, 1922, AGED 69 YR 1 MO 28 DA." The stone gives his birth year as 1848, a year earlier than every other documented source (1849) — likely a stonecutter's error, since the stone's own age-at-death figure (80 years, 6 months, 13 days) is arithmetically consistent with the documented 1849 birth date, not with 1848. This same discrepancy is already noted on Mary Ellen's own page.

Burial location — three sources, one outlier. His death certificate, Find A Grave, and two Titusville Herald items all record burial at Harrison Cemetery near Vrooman. Only the Erie Daily Times account (an out-of-town paper reporting from Spartansburg) names "Maple Grove cemetery" instead — treated here as a likely error in that one source, given the certificate's own explicit record, but stated rather than silently dropped.

Death certificate's "Name of Father" field is likely a clerical error. It reads "Will C. Morris" — but the informant on the same certificate is also "Will C. Morris" (his son, William E. "Will" Morris), and every other source (obituaries, Find A Grave, Ancestry) consistently names his father as Richard Morris. This reads as the clerk duplicating the informant's own name into the wrong field, not a real question about parentage.

Willis vs. William: the 12 July obituary names a surviving son "Willis Morris of Vrooman," while the Erie Daily Times gives "William, of Vroman." Both almost certainly refer to William E. "Will" Morris (1885–1966) — treated as the same person, name given as recorded in each source.
