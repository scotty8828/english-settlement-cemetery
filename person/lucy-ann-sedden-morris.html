Biography

Lucy Ann Sedden was born 7 November 1847, the daughter of Eliab "Eli" Sedden and Jane Harrison Sedden. She grew up alongside at least five siblings, including William Sedden, who would die in the Civil War in 1864, five years before Lucy Ann herself.

In 1866, Lucy Ann married Benjamin Morris — a son of Richard Morris and Jane Harrison Morris, and a brother of Private James Morris, who had died three years earlier of wounds received at Gettysburg. The marriage connected the Sedden family to the Morris family for a second time in a generation: Lucy Ann's own aunt, Sarah Ann Morris, had married Lucy Ann's uncle William Sedden a decade earlier. Lucy Ann and Benjamin had one son together, Frederick Wilson Morris, born in 1867.

Lucy Ann died on her monument's recorded date of 3 May 1869, at 21 years, 5 months, and 26 days old — though a Federal Mortality Schedule entry for Rome Township, found under "Morris, Lucy A.," raises a real possibility that her death actually occurred a year later, in May 1870: the schedule only covers deaths between June 1869 and May 1870, and its listed age of 22 lines up exactly with her monument's precise age if the death year is shifted forward by one. That same record gives her cause of death as consumption — tuberculosis — and her occupation as keeping house. Her death came in a year (whichever it precisely was) that claimed several other young people from the English Settlement, including four-year-old Abraham Lincoln Gregory and infant twins Albert and Alphord Harrison, all recorded as dying in 1869. No record here identifies a cause behind those other deaths, and if Lucy Ann's own death shifts to 1870, that particular coincidence may not hold — but the mortality schedule is worth pursuing further, as neighboring pages may hold more answers about the settlement in these years.

After Lucy Ann's death, her infant son Fred was raised by his grandmother, Jane Harrison Morris — a detail recorded in a family letter already documented elsewhere on this site, on Jane Harrison Sedden's biography page. Fred lived to 1938, a long life that began in grief.

Sources & notes
  • Find A Grave memorial #51923509 — birth, death, marriage, parents, siblings, and son; headstone photos by Rusty Davis and Virginia Bates-Hulings
  • Headstone inscription — exact age at death (21 years, 5 months, 26 days), confirming birth date
  • English Settlement Cemetery master record
  • U.S. Federal Census Mortality Schedule, 1850–1885 — Rome Township, Crawford County, PA, year ending 1 June 1870, line 6: "Morris, Lucy A.," age 22, cause of death consumption
This record resolves an open question from Jane Harrison Sedden's own page, which noted Lucy Ann "married one of Richard and Jane Harrison Morris's sons" without confirming which one — she married Benjamin Morris specifically, a brother of Private James Morris.
Possible one-year death date discrepancy: The 1870 Federal Mortality Schedule only records deaths occurring between June 1869 and May 1870. Lucy Ann's monument gives her death date as 3 May 1869 — a date that falls just before this schedule's window and would normally exclude her from it entirely. Yet a "Morris, Lucy A." appears on the schedule at age 22, died in May, of consumption. Her monument's very precise age at death (21 years, 5 months, 26 days from a birth date of 7 Nov 1847) matches exactly if her actual death date were 3 May 1870 rather than 1869 — same day and month, age advanced by exactly one year. This is a real possibility, not a confirmed correction: the death year shown here (1869) is Find A Grave's and the monument's, and has not been changed pending further evidence. Neighboring pages of this same mortality schedule may hold further information relevant to other English Settlement families and are worth reviewing.
