Biography

John M. Graham's exact birth date and birthplace are not documented; his stated age of 60 at death places his birth around 1836. He served in the Civil War in Company H of the 169th Pennsylvania Volunteers, and afterward belonged to the Pleasantville Post of the Grand Army of the Republic, the fraternal organization for Union veterans.

John Graham died 14 December 1896 at the residence of Peter Graham in Hydetown, Crawford County, of heart failure, at sixty years old. His relationship to Peter Graham, at whose home he died, has not been documented. He was survived by a wife and a thirteen-year-old son, neither yet identified by name.

His funeral was held at the United Brethren church at "Maple Grove," with burial "at the church cemetery of that place," according to his obituary — a striking detail, since "Maple Grove" is a name already documented elsewhere on this site as an early alternate name for the English Settlement itself. Whether the obituary refers to this same cemetery, or to a distinct church and burial ground elsewhere under the same name, is not yet resolved.

Additional biography will be added as research deepens. If you have information about John M. Graham, please use the contribution form below.

Obituary
Titusville Morning Herald · 16 December 1896
"John Graham, of English Settlement, died Monday at the residence of Peter Graham, of Hydetown, of heart failure, at the age of 60 years. Company H, 169 Pa. Vol. Veteran of the late war, a member of Pleasantville Post G.A.R. and highly respected. He is survived by a wife a son, age 13 years. Funeral took place from the U.B. church at Maple Grove, and interment will be at the church cemetery of that place."

Graham or Ingraham? An anonymous comment on his Find A Grave memorial notes that a separate cemetery index lists his surname as "Ingraham" rather than "Graham." This has not been resolved — it could reflect a transcription error in either the index or the monument itself, or the two names could genuinely differ across sources for reasons not yet understood.
A United Brethren church "at Maple Grove" — a new lead: This obituary is the first source encountered in this project's research to describe a physical U.B. church building "at Maple Grove," with its own churchyard cemetery. "Maple Grove" is already documented elsewhere on this site as an early alternate name for the English Settlement cemetery itself, confirmed independently by three separate sources. Whether this obituary's "church cemetery of that place" refers to the same English Settlement cemetery, or to a distinct church and burial ground that happened to share the name, is not yet resolved — worth investigating further, since it may bear on the still-deferred history of the cemetery's own name and origins.
Wife and son unnamed; Peter Graham's relationship unclear: Neither John's wife nor his thirteen-year-old son are named in the sources found so far. Peter Graham, at whose Hydetown residence John died, may be a relative — a son, brother, or other kin — but this has not been documented.
