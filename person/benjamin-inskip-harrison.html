Biography

Benjamin Inskip Harrison was born 28 November 1797 in Preston, Lancashire, England, the son of Benjamin Harrison and Jane Inskip Harrison. He was one of at least six children — alongside Richard, Elizabeth, Jane, Edward, and Inskip Harrison — born to a couple whose own emigration a generation later would help found the English Settlement.

A family memoir already documented on this site, describing the settlement's origins, names "the three heads of these families" who founded the English Settlement directly: Richard Morris, Inskip Harrison, and Benjamin Harrison. Benjamin — carrying his father's own first name, distinguished here by his middle name Inskip, his mother's maiden name — was one of these three founders, standing alongside his brother-in-law Richard Morris and his own brother Inskip Harrison to establish the settlement together.

Benjamin married twice. On 1 January 1818, at just 20 years old, he married Nancy Brown in Preston Parish, Lancashire — married by banns, with parental consent noted in the register, since Benjamin was still under the age of majority. Matthew Harrison, a relative not yet identified elsewhere in this family's records, stood as a witness, alongside Mary Pickering. The marriage took place in England, meaning Benjamin and Nancy were already married before the family's emigration to America. Together they had at least nine children over the following two decades: Jane, Benjamin, John Brown, Elizabeth "Betsy," Thomas, Sarah, Ellen, Richard Christopher, and Edward Inskip Harrison. Nancy died in 1840. Benjamin's second wife, Isabella Nesbitt, had already been widowed twice before — first as Isabella Duff, then as Isabella Edmond — and a census taken while she still headed the Edmond household shows her living with children from those earlier marriages, on the farm neighboring Benjamin's own. The same census shows Benjamin's household followed immediately by that of his daughter Jane and son-in-law William "Billie" Morris Sr., placing three generations of this family within a few farms of one another. Benjamin and Isabella had at least one child together, George Matthew Harrison, born in 1855 — Benjamin's youngest, arriving when he was 58 years old. Isabella died in 1875, the same year as Benjamin himself.

Several of Benjamin's children married into the Morris family, deepening the connection between the two founding lines still further: his daughter Jane Harrison married William "Billie" Morris Sr., and his daughter Ellen married Edward Morris in 1854 — Edward being Richard Morris's own eldest son, and the older brother of Private James Morris.

Benjamin died 23 October 1875 in Rome Township, at 77 years old, and was buried at English Settlement Cemetery — a founder of the community he helped build, laid to rest within it.

A 1915 published genealogical history of western Pennsylvania records Benjamin's trade: in England he had been a butcher — a skill likely learned from his father, Benjamin Harrison Sr., said in the same source to have practiced the trade in Bolton-by-Bowland, Lancashire — and after settling in Rome Township, he continued the work "to a certain extent," buying and selling livestock alongside farming his land.

Important find: Benjamin's daughter, Jane Harrison Morris (1818–1885), is very likely the same "Jane Harrison Morris" who married William "Billie" Morris Sr. and appears elsewhere on this site as a placeholder/demo page with her parentage unconfirmed. This record resolves that gap: her father is Benjamin Inskip Harrison, not Jane Inskip Harrison directly. This is the third distinct "Jane Harrison" identified across this family in two generations — a recurring naming pattern already flagged on multiple other pages. Her existing page on this site should be updated to reflect this parentage.
Benjamin's son Edward Inskip Harrison (1840–1914) is listed here as Benjamin's own son. This may be worth cross-checking against Jane Inskip Harrison's own biography page, which discusses the "Inskip" name passing down through the family — it's possible that page currently attributes Edward Inskip Harrison to a different line; not yet confirmed either way.
The census image is handwritten and some ages are difficult to read with full confidence; the household relationships and surnames (Duff, then Edmond, for Isabella's prior marriages; the Morris household immediately following Benjamin's) are clear, but exact ages should be treated as approximate pending a clearer copy or transcription.
Jordan's 1915 account gives Jane Inskip Harrison's dates as 1763–1848 — different from the 1766–1845 used consistently elsewhere on this site, including her own biography page, Find A Grave, and Ancestry.com records. This is a real discrepancy, not yet resolved; the dates here have not been changed to match Jordan's account, which stands as a single outlying source against several agreeing ones. Bolton-by-Bowland as the elder Benjamin Harrison's specific hometown in Lancashire is likewise sourced only to Jordan's account and has not yet been corroborated elsewhere.
