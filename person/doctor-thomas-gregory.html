Biography

Doctor Thomas Gregory was born on 4 September 1832 in New York City. "Doctor" was his given name, not a professional title — his death certificate lists his occupation plainly as farmer. He was the son of James Gregory (1789–1863) and Elizabeth Harrison Gregory (b. 1794), and the grandson, through his mother, of Benjamin and Jane Inskip Harrison, the settlement's founding couple.

He came to Crawford County with his parents at about eight years old, and spent nearly all the rest of his life in the same community — what his 1915 obituary called the "Windfall" district of Oil Creek Township, part of the broader Rome Township settlement his grandparents had helped establish a generation earlier. He farmed there for the whole of his working life and was, in the words of the local paper, "one of the best known and respected residents" of the district.

He married Elizabeth Ann Duff, with whom he raised at least eight children: sons Alexander, Christopher, James, Samuel, and Walter, and daughters Elizabeth, Mary Belle, and Jane Louise. His obituary named only four surviving sons — Christopher of Spartansburg, Alexander of Mansfield/Mansalonia, Michigan, Walter of Portland, New York, and James, still living at home — without mentioning Samuel, though family tree records show Samuel outliving his father by two decades; this gap in the obituary is not yet explained. Jane Louise, one of his daughters, died in 1914, a year before her father, and the obituary noted she "preceded her parents to the grave."

Doctor Thomas Gregory was widowed some years before his own death and lived out his final years at the family home, where he died on the night of 27 January 1915 of what his death certificate records as organic heart disease, of about three years' duration. His funeral was held at the family residence, with burial following in what the obituary calls "the Vrooman cemetery" — one of several historical names, along with Maple Grove and Harrison, that this same burying ground carried before settling on its present name, the English Settlement Cemetery.

Obituary
Titusville Herald · 29 January 1915

Doctor Thomas Gregory, nearly all of whose life was passed in this section and whose parents were among the pioneer settlers, died at the family home Wednesday night of infirmities incident to his advanced years. The deceased, who was one of the best known and respected residents of Oil Creek township in what is known as the "Windfall" district, was born in New York City, Sept. 4, 1830, and came to this section with his parents, James and Elizabeth Harrison Gregory, when but 8 years old, ever since then residing in the community in which he was honored and passed away.

Mr. Gregory was a widower, his wife passing away about four years ago, and he leaves to mourn his death four sons, Christopher Gregory of Spartansburg, Alexander Gregory of Mansalonia, Mich., Walter Gregory of Portland, N.Y., and James Gregory, living at home. He also leaves two daughters, Elizabeth Gregory of Queenstown and Belle Gregory of Grand Valley. Another daughter, Jane Louise, preceded her parents to the grave. Funeral service will be conducted at the family residence at 1 o'clock Saturday afternoon, with burial following in the Vrooman cemetery. The cemetery was first called Maple Grove, Vroman, Harrison, and last called the English Settlement Cemetery.

Three conflicts flagged, none resolved: (1) His obituary states he was born 4 Sept. 1830, but both his death certificate and Find A Grave give 4 Sept. 1832 — and the certificate's own age calculation (82 yrs, 4 mos, 23 days as of 27 Jan 1915) works backward to 1832, favoring that year. (2) Family tree data shows his mother, Elizabeth Harrison Gregory, dying in 1861, while the master record on this site gives 1881 — worth checking her own page. (3) His obituary states his wife "pass[ed] away about four years ago" (implying c.1911), but the master record gives her death as 1899 — a 12-year gap worth checking against her own page before either is corrected.

The obituary's cemetery-name note — "first called Maple Grove, Vroman, Harrison, and last called the English Settlement Cemetery" — is a strong independent confirmation of the site's ongoing research into the burying ground's historical names.
