Biography

George Nelson was born 3 August 1854, the son of Horace Fredrick "Fred" Nelson and Esther Emoline Olmstead Nelson. He was one of at least six children; his siblings Ida, Katie, and Otis were already born by the time of his death, and three more — Maud, Nora, and Otis — followed in the years after. The Nelson family was not part of the Harrison, Morris, Wheatall, Sedden, Gregory, or Crossley families otherwise documented on this site, but they were close enough to the community that word of George's death was carried directly to a member of the Harrison family.

On 24 October 1870, George was working at Howe's Mill alongside Henry Hipple, rolling a log off a skidway onto some planks, when the skidway broke. George was caught between two logs and crushed — his chest, feet, and ankles badly injured, and struck on the head by two planks, with it unclear at the time whether his neck was broken. He died of his injuries the following day, 25 October 1870, at sixteen years old. His funeral was held 27 October.

News of his death reached the family in a letter, quoted in full below, written the day of his funeral and addressed to "Dear Inskip" — very likely Inskip Harrison, a member of the settlement's founding family. The identity of "Mary," who wrote the letter, has not yet been confirmed.

George was buried at English Settlement Cemetery. His parents, Fred and Esther, are buried separately, at Excelsior Cemetery.

A letter home — reporting George's death, 27 October 1870
Written the day of George's funeral, addressed "Dear Inskip" — likely Inskip Harrison. The writer, "Mary," has not yet been identified.

Dear Inskip, I have some bad news to tell you. I have been to the funeral of George Nelson today. He was killed on the 24th day this month. Him and Henry Hipple was over to Howe's mill and they were rolling a log off the skidway onto some planks. The skidway broke and he was down between two logs and was killed. They say he was mashed a good deal — on his breast, another on his feet and ankles, and two planks hit him on the head. They did not know if his neck was broke or not. You will hear all about it when you get home.

— Mary
Transcribed on Find A Grave; headstone photos by Laura Priester and Virginia Bates-Hulings. Spelling lightly regularized for readability.

The identity of "Mary," who wrote the letter reporting his death, has not been confirmed; "Inskip," the recipient, is presumed to be Inskip Harrison (1812–1888) based on the name and the family's evident closeness to the Nelsons, but this is not yet independently verified.
