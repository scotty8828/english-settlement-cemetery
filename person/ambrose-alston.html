Biography

Ambrose Alston was born in England around 1824, the son of Thomas Alston (1796–1878) and Elizabeth Warrick Alston (1797–1878), who emigrated with their family and are buried at English Settlement Cemetery. Among his siblings was a sister, Jane Alston (1821–1869), who married David Crossley (1817–1880) and is also buried here; a brother, John Alston, is named in Ambrose's own will but has not otherwise been documented on this site.

By 1880 Ambrose was living in Titusville, Crawford County, in the household of his nephew James P. Crossley — son of his late sister Jane and her husband David Crossley — where the census recorded him, at age 61, as "Uncle" and gave his occupation as retired clock maker. James P. Crossley was then working as an oil producer; his household also included his wife, Allie, a young son named John P., and an infant daughter, Bertha J.

On 5 August 1893, still a resident of Titusville, Ambrose Alston made his last will and testament, directing that one third of his estate go to his brother John Alston, and the remaining two thirds be divided among the heirs of his late sister Jane Crossley — named as James P. Crossley, Jennie Bowman, and the children of the late Thos. [P./J.] Crossley. A codicil signed the same day made two further, more personal bequests, fitting for a man who had spent his working life among clocks: his large mahogany-cased clock was left to his nephew James P. Crossley, and his gold watch was left to James's son, John P., to be given to him when he reached the age of twenty-one. Ambrose named his friend Joseph J. Holden as sole executor, to serve without bond.

Ambrose Alston died on 11 November 1898 in Titusville, Crawford County, at about seventy-three or seventy-four years of age, and was buried at English Settlement Cemetery. His monument is a tall stone column set on a rectangular base inscribed ALSTON, standing among trees in a wooded section of the cemetery.

Additional biography — including his life and trade in England before emigration, and the circumstances of his brother John Alston — will be added as further research and Meadville-area records become available. If you have information about Ambrose Alston, please use the contribution form below.

Monument

No additional inscription is legible in the available photographs. A field visit and close examination by the museum have not yet been made.

Birth year conflict. Find A Grave lists Ambrose's birth as 1824 (aged 73–74 at death). The 1880 census gives his age as 61, which would put his birth around 1819. Not resolved.
Elizabeth Warrick Alston's death year conflict. This site's master record and Find A Grave place her death in 1878, alongside her husband Thomas Alston. An Ancestry family tree consulted for this page instead shows her death as 1893. This may affect her already-published page (#038) and has not been resolved.
Possible Ann Inskip connection — unconfirmed. The same Ancestry tree lists Ambrose's grandparents as Thomas Alston (1748–1822) and Ann Inskip (1753–1830) — a generation before the family's emigration. This is not yet confirmed by any primary source, but it is flagged here as a possible new lead relevant to the still-open question of whether an Ann/Anne Inskip was connected to Jane Inskip Harrison's family before emigration.
Probate registration date uncertain. The register notation on the will is difficult to read with certainty. Registration in November 1898 (shortly after Ambrose's death) is more consistent with normal probate practice than a date in 1893, but the original record should be re-checked to confirm.
"Thos. Crossley" identity unconfirmed. The will names the children of "the late Thos. [P./J.] Crossley" as co-heirs. This may be Thomas J. Crossley (buried here, 1844–1875, Civil War veteran), but the middle initial in the will transcription does not clearly match, and this has not been confirmed.
Marital status. No spouse or children have been found for Ambrose Alston in any source reviewed. His 1893 will divides his entire estate between his brother and his late sister's heirs, with no wife or children mentioned, but no source explicitly states that he never married.
