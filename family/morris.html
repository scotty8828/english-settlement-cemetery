The family story

The Morris family's American story begins with a Lancashire blacksmith's prison sentence. James Morris (1782–1858), born a twin at his family's farm in Haslingden Grane, was convicted in 1820 of manufacturing pikeheads for the parliamentary reform movement in the aftermath of the Peterloo Massacre, and spent twenty months imprisoned at Lancaster Castle. According to family memory, it was that experience — and a wish for "a government to their own liking" — that drove him to bring his family to America afterward.

James's son Richard Morris (1810–1871) married Jane Harrison, daughter of the Harrison family's founding matriarch, in 1830. In 1833, Richard — together with Jane's brothers Benjamin Inskip and Inskip Harrison — purchased 400 acres in Rome Township and helped found what became known as the English Settlement. James and the rest of the family followed later, once his own children's marriages had pulled him toward the same community.

The Morris and Harrison families became deeply intertwined from that point on, marrying into each other repeatedly across generations — a pattern this project has spent considerable effort untangling, given how often the same names (James, William, John, Benjamin) repeat across both families.

This page is a starting point, not a finished history. As more individual Morris biographies are built — including the many descendants who carried the family through Vrooman, Titusville, and beyond — they'll be added below.
